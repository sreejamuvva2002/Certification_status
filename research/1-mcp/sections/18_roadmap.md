# 18. Six-phase development roadmap

Sequenced so the cheapest experiment that can kill the project runs first. Each
phase has an explicit exit test; failing it means stop or redesign, not proceed
with caveats.

## Phase 1 — Feasibility screening (2–4 weeks)

**Question: does a paper-borne 1-MCP complex survive manufacture and release
measurable gas into a headspace?**

Minimum experiments:

1. Assay the as-received complex for actual 1-MCP content. *This single number
   determines whether any of the dose arithmetic is real.*
2. Establish the GC-FID method: calibration with mass balance, LOD/LOQ, blank
   substrate, leak test.
3. Make Architecture C·0 (complex on absorbent paper, no binder) and measure
   headspace release at high and low RH.
4. Coat three binders (ethanolic/shellac, PEG, water-based) by Meyer rod; assay
   retained 1-MCP after drying.

**Run in parallel, not as an experiment: a conversation with a FIFRA regulatory
specialist** about whether this format needs a new registration and whether a
registrant partner is a precondition. This is the highest-value action in Phase 1
and it is not a lab activity.

**Exit test:** measurable, quantifiable headspace 1-MCP from a coated sample, with
retention after coating and drying materially better than the water-based control.
**Kill if** no route retains a usable fraction of the charge (FM01).

## Phase 2 — Formulation optimisation (4–8 weeks)

Optimise binder, coat weight (≥3 levels), complex loading, and the protective
overwrap. Begin the pre-activation storage-stability study **on day one of this
phase** — it runs longest and gates commercialisation independently.

**Exit test:** a formulation with reproducible coat weight and acceptable payload
retention over a realistic storage window in its overwrap. **Kill if** payload
cannot be held in storage (FM02).

## Phase 3 — Release-kinetics validation (6–10 weeks)

The full protocol of Section 11: RH matrix × temperature matrix, replicates,
mass balance, sacrificial-vial sampling, model fitting.

**This phase decides whether the project has a differentiator.** The two figures
that matter are release-fraction-versus-RH (is there a threshold?) and delivered
concentration across varying package volume (is dose reproducible?).

**Exit test:** a defensible release model, a demonstrated RH threshold, and a
stated dose-reproducibility CV. **Kill if** release versus RH is smooth and
shallow across all binders, or CV stays high after optimisation (FM05, FM06) —
in that case there is no specification to claim and no product to differentiate.

## Phase 4 — Produce validation (one season, or 6–12 weeks on tomato)

The design of Section 13 on tomato, with all eight treatment groups including
conventional gaseous 1-MCP as a positive control. Peach follows in season if
Phase 3 succeeded and the antimicrobial arm is being pursued.

**Exit test:** quality retention significantly better than untreated and than
matrix-only controls, with no off-odour or disorder. **Kill if** the insert
underperforms the conventional positive control by a margin that a dose increase
cannot close within cost and sensory limits.

## Phase 5 — Scale-up demonstration (8–12 weeks)

Lab Meyer rod → pilot slot-die at matched coat weight, ideally toll-coated on an
existing line. Compare release curves lab versus pilot directly — the reason both
are specified in g·m⁻².

**Exit test:** pilot material reproduces lab release behaviour within the
tolerance implied by the dose specification. **Kill if** it does not (FM17); a
formulation that only works at bench scale is not a product.

## Phase 6 — Patent and commercialisation assessment (parallel from Phase 3)

Invention disclosure with the claim-element matrix and the prior-art map naming
Cao et al. and the Cellresin family explicitly; updated prior-art search
including unexamined applications; FTO review by counsel; cost model refreshed
with a real complex quotation; regulatory consultation concluded.

**Exit test:** counsel identifies a claimable position with a practicable
design-around, and regulatory advice identifies a viable route to market.
**Kill if** either fails (FM13, FM14).

## Critical-path note

Phases 1 and 6 both start immediately. The regulatory and patent questions are
capable of ending the project independently of every experimental result, and
they have the longest lead times. Running them last — the usual instinct — is how
projects spend a year on formulation before discovering they cannot sell the
product.

## Honest assessment of the schedule

The lab work is roughly 6–9 months to the end of Phase 5 with one full-time
researcher, assuming the complex is available and the GC is accessible. The
regulatory and IP path is likely longer and is the binding constraint on
commercialisation, not the science.
