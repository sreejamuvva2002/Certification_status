# 19. The ten most important unanswered questions

Ordered by how much the answer would change what happens next. The first three
can end the project and none of them is a laboratory experiment.

**1. Does a coated-paper 1-MCP insert require a new FIFRA registration, and is a
registrant partner a precondition for commercialisation?**
Nothing else matters if the answer is unfavourable. 1-MCP is a registered
pesticide active (EPA Reg. 71297-2, 2002) and registrations are crop- and
format-specific — SmartFresh InBox is verified as approved for kiwifruit only in
California. *Resolve by: regulatory specialist consultation, Phase 1.*

**2. Is there a practicable design-around of the Cellresin / Kimberly-Clark
claims, and what exactly does Fresh Inset's Vidre+ patent cover?**
`US10212931B2` claims a cyclodextrin composition coated on a substrate with a
second substrate over it. Vidre+ is stated by its owner to be patented for
delayed, gradual 1-MCP release. Both sit directly across the proposed position.
*Resolve by: claim-by-claim review and FTO search by counsel.*

**3. What is the actual 1-MCP content of the α-CD complex, and what does it
cost?**
The techno-economic model spans roughly an order of magnitude on cost per insert
almost entirely because of this one unverified input, and the dose arithmetic in
Section 9.2 rests on an assumed 3 % w/w payload. *Resolve by: supplier quotation
plus assay. Cheapest high-value action in the project.*

**4. Does release fraction versus relative humidity show a genuine threshold, or
only a smooth gradient?**
This is the technical question the differentiator stands on. A smooth gradient
means "humidity-triggered" can be described but not *specified*, and the product
collapses into an undifferentiated slow-release insert. *Resolve by: Phase 3
release programme.*

**5. How reproducible is the delivered headspace concentration across realistic
variation in package volume, produce mass and temperature?**
A dose specification is the proposed contribution. No retrieved source — academic
or commercial — reports this. *Resolve by: Phase 3, reported as a coefficient of
variation.*

**6. How much 1-MCP survives each coating route, quantitatively?**
Cao et al. adopted a water-free method on the reasoning that moisture discharges
the complex, but the *relationship* between process water content and retained
payload appears nowhere. It is both a design input and a candidate patentable
result. *Resolve by: Phase 1 binder screen with retention assay.*

**7. What is the gap between the minimum inhibitory vapour concentration of
carvacrol in a real package and its sensory detection threshold on the target
fruit?**
This single number decides whether the antimicrobial arm is viable at all. It is
commodity-specific and no verified value was obtained here. *Resolve by: paired
in-package challenge test and sensory panel, before any v2 formulation work.*

**8. What do the incumbent products actually cost per package?**
No published pricing for SmartFresh InBox, HarvestHold Fresh, Vidre+ or Hazel 100
was retrievable, so the cost model has an internal figure and **no verified
comparison**. Any claim of being cheaper is currently unsupported. *Resolve by:
distributor quotation.*

**9. Does an essential oil measurably displace 1-MCP from cyclodextrin, or
partition it into an oil phase?**
Section 7.3 argues both are plausible from first principles, but no retrieved
study measured either directly. If the effect is negligible, single-layer
dual-active designs become viable and the architecture calculus changes. *Resolve
by: a small headspace experiment — complex with and without oil contact.*

**10. What lies in the literature the four inaccessible indexes cover?**
Web of Science, Scopus, CAB Abstracts and AGRICOLA were unavailable. CAB
Abstracts and AGRICOLA in particular index postharvest, extension and conference
material that OpenAlex and Europe PMC cover unevenly — exactly where a 1-MCP
paper insert might have been described without appearing here. *Resolve by: a
search through an institutional subscription. Until then, every statement in this
report about the absence of prior work is weaker than it appears.*

## A note on questions 1, 2 and 8

Three of the ten are not scientific questions, and two of those three are capable
of ending the project on their own. That distribution is itself a finding: **the
principal risks to this project are regulatory, legal and commercial, not
technical.** The technical work is tractable and cheap by comparison. Resourcing
the project as though the hard part is the chemistry would be a mistake.
