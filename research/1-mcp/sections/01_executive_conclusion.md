# 1. Executive conclusion

## 1.1 The short answer

**The broad idea is not novel, and the closest prior art is closer than the brief
assumed.** A 2024 preprint (Cao et al., Nanjing Forestry University) already
combines carvacrol/β-cyclodextrin **and** 1-MCP/α-cyclodextrin on **coated paper**
made by a **water-free method**, validated on **peach**. Separately, the coated-
insert *format* is commercial — Verdant Technologies' HarvestHold Fresh is a
1-MCP sheet applied in the box — and delayed, gradual in-package 1-MCP release is
commercially claimed by Fresh Inset (Vidre+). And **Verdant Technologies — which sells HarvestHold Fresh and now holds the
Cellresin estate — has an application claiming a coated substrate whose coating
consists essentially of a 1-MCP clathrate of α-cyclodextrin**, which is the
recommended architecture in claim language.

**The proposed starting formulation is worse than the simpler alternative.** A
chitosan/CNC film carrying a 1-MCP/cyclodextrin complex *and* a CNC-stabilised
essential-oil Pickering emulsion scores **last of six architectures** (43/100),
because its central feature — an aqueous, hygroscopic matrix — attacks the one
thing the product must protect: a payload that water discharges.

**What remains defensible is narrow, quantitative, and worth testing.** Not a new
format, not a new material set, and not "controlled release" in the abstract, but
a **specified humidity activation threshold and coat weight delivering a
reproducible headspace 1-MCP concentration across varying package volume and
fruit mass**, demonstrated by GC quantification and a fitted model. No retrieved
source — academic or commercial — reports a coat weight, a release model, an
activation threshold, or a dose-reproducibility figure for a 1-MCP paper.

**The principal risks are not technical, and one may already be decisive.** The
patent estate is systematically enclosed by three parties — Verdant, AgroFresh
and Hazel — covering the complex, the film, the coating, the coated substrate and
the dual active. Of the ten most important open questions, three are regulatory,
legal or commercial, and two can end the project regardless of any experimental
result. 1-MCP is a registered
pesticide active (EPA Reg. 71297-2, since 2002) and registrations are crop- and
format-specific.

## 1.2 Direct answers to the brief's fifteen questions

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the broad idea novel? | **No.** Anticipated in the literature, in patents, and in commercial products. |
| 2 | Which parts are already known? | All of them: 1-MCP/CD complexes, humidity activation, coated paper and sheet inserts, labels and sachets, dual 1-MCP + essential oil on paper, and delayed release. |
| 3 | Where is the most credible white space? | **Dose specification and measurement** — a defined RH threshold and coat weight giving reproducible headspace concentration across variable packages. Measured across 82 papers: only 4 report a kinetic model, 4 report package volume and mass, 9 report loading. But this is a *method* claim in an article space that is already enclosed, so it is weak-to-moderate at best. |
| 4 | Film, coated paper, label, patch, insert, pad or sachet? | **Coated paper insert** — highest score on every project-critical criterion. Chosen as the right engineering answer, not as a novel format. |
| 5 | Same layer or separate layers? | **Separate — and omit the oil in v1.** Cavity competition, oil-phase partitioning, plasticisation, and divergent release requirements all argue against co-location. |
| 6 | Is CNC necessary? | **No.** With no oil phase to stabilise it has no function; it adds cost and process steps. |
| 7 | Is chitosan necessary? | **No.** Hygroscopic (wrong for a moisture-triggered payload), needs acidic aqueous processing, and its contact-antimicrobial mechanism cannot operate in a non-contact insert. |
| 8 | Is the Pickering emulsion justified? | **No.** It requires the aqueous phase the design exists to avoid. Cao et al. achieved a dual-active coated paper without one. |
| 9 | Which antimicrobial is best? | **Carvacrol**, if any — pure compound, documented against *Monilinia*, and directly comparable to the closest prior art. Deferred to v2. |
| 10 | Which commodity first? | **Tomato** (fast, cheap, unambiguous response), then **peach** (commercial relevance, decay pressure, direct comparability). |
| 11 | Simplest proof-of-concept? | 1-MCP/α-CD + one binder on paper, Meyer-rod coated, protective overwrap. **Three materials.** |
| 12 | Most scalable route? | **Meyer-rod in the lab → slot-die at scale**, both specified in g·m⁻². Reject extrusion coating (thermal loss) and solvent casting (no scale-up path, unscalable units). |
| 13 | Principal patent risk? | **`US20210331990A1` (Verdant, priority 2020-04-27)** — claims a coated substrate whose coating consists essentially of a 1-MCP clathrate of α-cyclodextrin. That is the recommended architecture, and it needs no second substrate, so the single-layer design-around does not clear it. |
| 14 | What justifies continuing? | A **sharp, reproducible RH activation threshold** plus a **low delivered-dose CV** across package volumes — evidence nobody currently publishes. |
| 15 | What should cause abandonment? | Release versus RH smooth and shallow across all binders; or dose CV that stays high after optimisation; or a blocking claim with no design-around; or a registration requirement beyond project resources. |

## 1.3 What we recommend

**Primary:** a single-layer 1-MCP/α-CD coated paper insert with a protective
overwrap. Three materials, one coating pass, mild drying, no fruit contact.

**Backup:** complex adsorbed on absorbent paper with no binder at all — two
materials, built to answer one question fast.

**High-risk/high-reward:** a condensate-managing coated pad that uses the moisture
it manages as its trigger.

**Do first, before any formulation work:** assay the complex's actual 1-MCP
content, get a supplier quotation, and ask a FIFRA specialist whether this
product can reach market without a registrant partner.

## 1.4 How much confidence this deserves

The evidence base is real and verifiable — every value in the literature and
patent tables is traceable to a quote in a document that was actually retrieved,
and values that could not be verified are marked rather than guessed. But three
limitations should temper any conclusion about *absence* of prior work:

* **Four indexes named in the brief were inaccessible** (Web of Science, Scopus,
  CAB Abstracts, AGRICOLA). CAB Abstracts and AGRICOLA index precisely the
  postharvest and extension literature where a 1-MCP paper insert might be
  described.
* **Patent coverage rests on a single provider**, with no independent legal-status
  cross-check, and unexamined applications were not searchable.
* **Open-access coverage of this literature is thin** — 34 of 82 extracted papers
  yielded full text, so of 2 050 table cells, 265 are full-text-verified, 248
  abstract-level, 454 confirmed *not stated*, and 837 unverifiable. Process-detail
  cells are often legitimately empty rather than genuinely absent from the source.

Nothing here constitutes a freedom-to-operate or patentability opinion, and none
should be inferred.
