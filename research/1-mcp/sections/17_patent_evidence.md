# 17. Patent-oriented evidence plan

**No opinion on patentability or freedom to operate is offered.** This section
sets out what evidence would be worth putting in front of a technology-transfer
office, and — equally important — what would not.

## 17.1 Separating the layers of the claim

| Layer | Status |
|---|---|
| **Broad idea** — humidity-triggered 1-MCP release from packaging, optionally with an essential oil, for climacteric fruit | **Not novel.** Disclosed in the literature, claimed in patents, and sold commercially |
| **Format** — coated paper insert / sheet / label | **Not novel.** HarvestHold Fresh is a sheet applied in the box; Vidre+ is a sticker |
| **Materials** — 1-MCP/α-CD on paper with an essential-oil/β-CD co-active | **Not novel.** Cao et al. (2024) |
| **Delayed / gradual release** | **Claimed commercially** (Vidre+) and titled in `US20210331990A1` |
| **Potentially differentiating feature** | A **specified humidity activation threshold and release window**, set by defined binder and coat weight, delivering a **reproducible headspace 1-MCP concentration across varying package volume and produce mass** |
| **Evidence required** | Sections 17.2–17.3 |
| **Closest prior art** | Cao et al. 2024 (`10.21203/rs.3.rs-4251760/v1`); `US10212931B2` (Kimberly-Clark/Cellresin); `US20210331990A1`; HarvestHold Fresh; Vidre+ |
| **Proposed design-around** | Avoid the second-substrate laminate of `US10212931B2`; claim a *specification and method* rather than an article format |
| **Remaining legal uncertainty** | **High.** Requires claim-by-claim review, an FTO search by counsel, and a search of unexamined applications this review could not access |

## 17.2 Results that would strengthen a disclosure

Ranked by how much they would actually add, given what is already public:

**Strong — these are genuinely absent from the retrieved prior art:**

1. **A sharp RH activation threshold.** A step change in release fraction over a
   narrow RH band, reproducible across batches. This is the difference between
   "humidity-triggered" as a description and as a specification. No retrieved
   source demonstrates it.
2. **A quantitative coat-weight → release-rate relationship.** A designed,
   monotonic, predictive relationship in g·m⁻². No retrieved source reports coat
   weight at all for a 1-MCP paper.
3. **Dose reproducibility across varying package volume and fruit mass**, reported
   with a coefficient of variation. This is what a commercial buyer needs and what
   nobody publishes.
4. **A measured retention-versus-processing-water relationship** — retained 1-MCP
   as a function of binder water content and drying condition. Supports a
   low-temperature/low-water manufacturing claim with numbers rather than an
   assertion.

**Moderate:**

5. A reproducible, tunable lag time before onset (weaker than it looks — Vidre+
   claims delayed release commercially).
6. Improved pre-activation shelf stability versus an uncoated or unwrapped
   control, quantified.
7. Equivalent efficacy at lower 1-MCP loading than a commercial benchmark.

**Weak — do not build a case on these:**

8. "It works on tomato/peach." Efficacy of 1-MCP on climacteric fruit is
   thoroughly established; demonstrating it again adds nothing.
9. "Antimicrobial plus ethylene control." Cao et al. did this. Only a
   *demonstrated interaction* — measured against both single-active controls —
   would add anything, and Section 7 argues the interaction is more likely to be
   negative than synergistic.
10. Materials characterisation (FTIR/XRD/SEM confirming an inclusion complex).
    Standard, expected, and not distinguishing.

## 17.3 Minimum evidence package for a TTO conversation

Deliberately small — enough to make a decision, not a thesis:

1. **Release curves** at ≥3 RH levels × ≥2 temperatures, ≥4 replicates, with
   full mass balance and a stated analytical method including LOD/LOQ.
2. **A threshold plot**: release fraction versus RH, resolved finely enough to
   show whether a step exists. *This is the single most important figure.*
3. **A coat-weight series**: ≥3 coat weights, same formulation, release rate
   versus g·m⁻².
4. **A dose-reproducibility table**: delivered headspace concentration across ≥3
   package volumes and ≥2 fruit masses, with CV.
5. **A retention table**: retained 1-MCP after each process route (water-based,
   PEG, ethanolic) and after storage in the intended overwrap.
6. **One produce trial** with all essential controls, including conventional
   gaseous 1-MCP as a positive control.
7. **This prior-art map**, with Cao et al. and the Cellresin family named
   explicitly rather than omitted.

Point 7 matters. Presenting a disclosure that does not name its closest prior art
wastes the TTO's time and damages credibility when they find it — and they will.

## 17.4 What would tell you to stop

* Release versus RH is smooth and shallow across every binder → there is no
  engineerable threshold, and the differentiator does not exist.
* Replicate CV in delivered concentration stays high after optimisation → no dose
  specification can be written, which was the whole claim.
* Counsel identifies a blocking claim with no practicable design-around.

Any of these should end the patent track. The engineering may still be worth
doing as a product-development or teaching exercise, but not as an IP play.

## 17.5 A note on the preprint

Cao et al. (2024) is a **Research Square preprint**, not a peer-reviewed article.
That affects how much weight to give its *data*, but **not** its status as prior
art — public disclosure is public disclosure regardless of peer review. It should
be treated as fully citable prior art and checked for a subsequent journal
version before any filing.
