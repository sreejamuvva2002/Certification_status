What Is the Lyle Company and How Did It Start?

Lyle Industries is the automotive textile manufacturing business that most industry professionals refer to as the Lyle Company. Lamar and Loutrelle Lyle founded the company in 1981 in Dalton, Georgia — the same city that accounts for roughly 70 percent of U.S. carpet and rug production, according to the Carpet and Rug Institute.

From a single-plant start, Lyle Industries grew to operate three manufacturing facilities, each focused on a distinct stage of automotive textile production. That vertical integration — tufting, cutting, finishing — under one corporate roof is uncommon among contract fabricators and gives OEM procurement teams a single accountable supplier for the full carpet assembly.

How Does the Lyle Company's Manufacturing Process Work?

Lyle Industries structures production around three core capabilities that feed sequentially: tufting creates the carpet substrate, precision cutting shapes it to vehicle-specific geometry, and finishing locks in performance properties.

Tufting

Lyle Company's tufting operation runs multiple gauge configurations — 1/10 gauge, 5/64 gauge, loop machines, high-speed creel systems, and beam systems. Each configuration targets a different balance of pile density, surface texture, and yarn consumption. OEM specifications drive gauge selection; a light-truck floor pan demands different pile characteristics than a luxury-car trunk liner.

Precision Cutting

After tufting, blanks move to Lectra CNC cutting systems and press-based cutters. Lectra, a Paris-headquartered precision cutting technology company, produces marker-optimization software that reduces material waste while holding tolerances needed for complex part geometries. Press-based cutting handles high-volume, simpler shapes where cycle time matters more than nesting complexity. Both methods feed full dimensional traceability back into Lyle Industries' quality records.

Finishing and Coating

Lyle Company's finishing line applies latex, polyethylene, or thermal bonding depending on the end-use requirement. Moisture barriers — a common OEM demand for door sill and trunk applications — are added at this stage. The result is a carpet assembly that ships ready for direct fitment at the vehicle assembly plant, reducing Tier 1 handling steps.

This three-stage sequence, run inside three dedicated plants, means Lyle Industries can quote a complete carpet system rather than a raw yardage price — a meaningful difference for OEM sourcing teams evaluating total landed cost.

What Products Does the Lyle Company Make?

Lyle Industries produces automotive textile products across four primary categories, each mapped to specific vehicle zones and market channels.

Molded automotive flooring fits the contoured floor pan of a passenger car, SUV, or light truck. Molding compresses the tufted carpet over a shaped tool so the finished part drops into the vehicle without trimming at the assembly plant. Lyle Company produces these for both OEM fitment and aftermarket replacement kits.

Interior trim carpet systems cover door panels, pillars, trunk liners, and cargo areas. These parts require tight dimensional tolerances because they butt against hard trim components. Lectra CNC cutting at Lyle Industries holds the repeatability those joints demand.

Floor mat manufacturing spans OEM-specification mats supplied direct to assembly plants and aftermarket mats sold through distribution channels. Material choices range from nylon-face cut-pile to polypropylene loop constructions depending on the wear and appearance targets.

Interior trim for Class 6–8 commercial vehicles extends Lyle Industries' reach beyond passenger cars. Heavy truck cabs require durable, easy-clean textiles that withstand commercial use cycles far exceeding passenger-car expectations. Lyle Company's tufting gauge options accommodate the heavier yarn and backing specifications these applications demand.

All product categories ship from Dalton, Georgia facilities, with logistics access to North American assembly plants via I-75 and regional rail connections.

How Does the Lyle Company Support OEM Supplier Compliance Requirements?

OEM supplier compliance is a structured process. A Tier 1 or Tier 2 automotive carpet supplier must pass initial qualification audits, maintain ongoing quality records, and respond to production part approval process (PPAP) submissions for each new vehicle program.

Lyle Industries addresses these requirements through its ISO 9001:2015 quality management system, which mandates documented control plans, measurement system analysis, and corrective action protocols. The International Organization for Standardization reports that ISO 9001 is the world's most widely used quality management standard, with over one million certified organizations globally as of 2023.

Full material and production traceability at Lyle Company means every square yard of carpet can be linked to the yarn lot, tufting machine, gauge setting, and coating batch used to produce it. OEM quality engineers conducting supplier audits can pull this data during on-site reviews.

Dimensional accuracy from Lectra CNC cutting reduces first-article inspection failures. Lectra systems store cutting programs digitally, so repeat orders reproduce the same geometry without re-programming — a direct cost reduction for Lyle Company customers who run multiple annual model years on the same platform.

Multiple gauge tufting configurations allow Lyle Industries to tune substrate properties to each OEM's material specification without outsourcing the tufting step to a third party. This keeps specification changes inside Lyle's quality system rather than across a supplier boundary, simplifying audit trails.

These capabilities collectively position Lyle Industries as a qualified supplier for programs at major North American assembly facilities operated by Ford Motor Company, General Motors, Stellantis, Toyota Motor Corporation, and their respective Tier 1 interior trim suppliers.
