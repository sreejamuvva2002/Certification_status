    Ford, GM clash over trade policies as Detroit rivalry heats up Ford and GM are backing a unified North American trade strategy while pushing for tariff policies that better fit their individual supply chains. Ford is seeking higher tariffs on South Korean vehicles and lower duties on imported aluminum, while saying roughly 80% of the vehicles it sells in the U.S. are built domestically. GM, meanwhile, produces about 400,000 vehicles annually for the U.S. market and is focused on protecting its own manufacturing and technology investments. The differences extend beyond tariffs. GM is challenging Ford’s reliance on Chinese battery technology through its Michigan venture with CATL, while pursuing its own battery development, including sodium-based technology. Both automakers support renewing USMCA and favor lower tariffs for North American-built vehicles and parts, but their diverging strategies around EVs, batteries, defense and vehicle production are increasingly shaping their trade priorities.

    🚗 North America’s Auto Supply Chain Just Got Another Shock The U.S. automotive industry is facing fresh uncertainty after President Trump threatened to raise tariffs on Canadian-made cars, trucks, and auto parts to 50% from January 1, 2027, following the breakdown of U.S.–Canada trade negotiations. This isn't simply a tariff story. It's a supply-chain story. The North American automotive industry is deeply integrated, with vehicles and components crossing borders multiple times during production. A major tariff change could therefore affect manufacturers, parts suppliers, logistics providers, and ultimately vehicle costs. What could change? 🔩 Auto Components — Suppliers may need to reconsider production and sourcing locations. 🏭 Manufacturing Footprints — Automakers could accelerate investments in domestic production. 🚢 Trade Routes — Cross-border shipment patterns could shift as companies search for more cost-efficient alternatives. 📦 Supplier Networks — Manufacturers may increasingly diversify their sourcing to reduce tariff exposure. 💰 Cost Pressure — Higher duties can create additional costs throughout the automotive supply chain. The bigger question for businesses isn't just “What tariff comes next?” It's: Which suppliers, countries, products and trade routes will gain from the reshuffling? That's where trade intelligence becomes valuable.
