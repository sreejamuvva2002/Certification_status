Korean Tire Producers Face Crisis with High U.S. Tariffs on Export Base Countries

  • Editor Michael Herh
  • 2025.04.04 18:10
Share the articles

Hankook Accelerates Expansion of Tennessee Plant; Kumho Adjusts Production in Korea and Europe

U.S. President Donald Trump's decision to impose hefty tariffs on major ASEAN countries has impacted domestic tire companies. Kumho Tire has been exporting tires produced in Vietnam, and Hankook Tire from Indonesia, to the U.S., achieving record performance. However, with high tariffs looming, both companies are establishing emergency measures such as expanding U.S. factories and adjusting global supply chains to minimize tariff damage.

The top two domestic tire companies, Hankook Tire and Kumho Tire, are facing significantly increased tariffs on tires exported from Southeast Asian production plants to the U.S. The U.S. has announced a 25% tariff on automotive parts, including tires, starting from April 3, which will result in higher tariffs on export volumes to the U.S.

Due to the Trump administration's tariff imposition, Hankook Tire and Kumho Tire could face losses amounting to hundreds of billions of won. Kumho Tire produces approximately 3.3 million tires at its Georgia plant in the U.S., but most of the tires sold in the U.S. are exported from its Binh Duong plant in Vietnam (13.5 million tires). Last year, 80% of its North American sales, amounting to approximately 1.11 trillion won, relied on exports.

Particularly, 95% of its exports to the U.S. are manufactured in Vietnam, previously subject to only a 7.89% tariff. If the tire tariff rate rises to 25%, the importer's burden will increase by 178 billion won, which Kumho Tire may have to absorb significantly.

The situation for Hankook Tire is similar. Although it has a plant in Tennessee capable of producing about 5 million tires, most of its sales in the U.S. are from imported products. Last year, 75% of its North American sales, amounting to 1.755 trillion won, were from imported products. Half of its exports to the U.S. are produced in Indonesia, where the current tariff rate is only 4%. Starting next month, with a 25% tariff, Hankook Tire will have to bear an additional 184 billion won in tariffs with importers, based on last year's U.S. sales.

The concern is that the tariff bomb scenario could worsen. The Trump administration has not yet decided whether to impose a 25% tariff on Vietnamese and Indonesian tires as automotive parts or to apply reciprocal tariffs. If reciprocal tariffs are applied, the tariff rate could soar to 46% for Vietnamese tires and 32% for Indonesian products.

Hankook and Kumho Tire are preparing countermeasures assuming the worst-case scenario. Hankook Tire plans to speed up the expansion of its Tennessee plant to increase production to 12 million tires. A company official stated, "We will minimize short-term damage while expanding the Tennessee plant to increase production volume."

Kumho Tire is adjusting its global production bases. If reciprocal tariffs are imposed on Vietnamese export products, it plans to replace U.S. export volumes using its plants in Korea and Europe. A Kumho Tire representative said, "The U.S. is a key market with high demand for SUVs and large-inch tires," and added, "We will reduce tariff damage through flexible supply chain adjustments."
