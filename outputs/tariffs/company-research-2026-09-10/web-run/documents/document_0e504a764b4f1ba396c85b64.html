As I’m sure there are many questions in the trucking space today about the tariffs on heavy trucks, below are data to help put things in perspective. Thoughts: •The table shows the imports of heavy trucks (NAICS 336120) on a customs value basis in 2024. Mexico was by far the largest source at $15.6 billion (71.9%), with Canada a clear second at $4.5 billion (20.7%). As such, I’m confident these tariffs will override USMCA, just as finished vehicles from Canada and Mexico are now tariffed regardless of USMCA compliance. •The bottom chart shows the share of domestic consumption of heavy trucks that imports represent. This is calculated as imports / (imports + domestic manufacturing – domestic exports), where domestic manufacturing output comes from the M3 Census Bureau data and domestic exports represent the value of US heavy trucks that were exported. Since 2012, that number has consistently been between 35-40% (not sure how this then represents a national security concern if most of our domestic consumption is met with domestic production). •In terms of medium and heavy truck production, production levels in the USA the last few years have been around 330,000 units (see https://lnkd.in/gygFWpPN). Implication: while the BBB bring back 100% bonus depreciation helps trucking companies planning on investing in equipment, these heavy truck tariffs, in conjunction with the tariffs on Mexican and Canadian trailers through the steel and aluminum derivatives, are heavily unwelcome for an industry whose profitability has been poor for the last three years. #supplychain #freight #trucking #truckload #transportation #logistics

    President Trump continues to expand tariffs sector-by-sector rather than country-by-country. He recently announced that, starting November 1st, all medium- and heavy-duty trucks imported into the United States will face a 25% tariff. In a recent article published by Transport Topics, two industry leaders shared their concerns: 🚚 International Motors, which manufactures trucks both domestically and abroad, stated: “Export and import restrictions can lead to severe disruptions in the supply chain, affecting the availability of trucks and truck parts in the U.S. This can result in freight delays, increased costs, and operational challenges for businesses reliant on shipping or receiving these products.” 🚚 Hirschbach Motor Lines, a 90-year-old transportation services provider, added: “The import of medium- and heavy-duty trucks and truck parts is not a threat to the national security interests of the United States, but imposing tariffs that drive up the cost of these vehicles will adversely affect our business and the U.S. customers and communities we serve.” Medium- and heavy-duty trucks are categorized by their Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR): - Medium-duty: 14,001–26,000 pounds - Heavy-duty: 26,001+ pounds Medium-duty trucks are typically used for regional or local deliveries and services, while heavy-duty trucks are designed for long-haul freight and transporting heavy machinery. According to the U.S. Commerce Department, approximately 245,000 medium- and heavy-duty trucks worth $20 billion were imported into the U.S. last year.

    🚛 To boost U.S. manufacturing, President Donald Trump signed an executive order that will take effect on November 1, imposing new tariffs on heavy-duty trucks, medium-duty trucks, and buses. The move aims to reduce reliance on imported vehicles and parts while encouraging production inside the United States. 📜 What’s Changing Heavy and medium trucks: 25% import tariff Buses: 10% import tariff Key parts like engines, transmissions, tires, and chassis will also face the 25% rate. These tariffs cover Class 3 to Class 8 vehicles — from school buses and city transit buses to pickup trucks, dump trucks, and 18-wheelers. 🏛️ Why It Matters The White House says the goal is national security. Trucks carry over 70% of U.S. freight, moving food, fuel, and medical supplies nationwide. Because many of these vehicles are imported, the administration wants to ensure the U.S. can build and supply its own fleets in times of crisis. 💰 Relief Measures To soften the impact, companies that qualify under the U.S.–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA) can apply for tariff reductions. Manufacturers that assemble vehicles in the U.S. will also get 3.75% in annual tariff relief for five years, up to a total of 18.75%. 📈 Broader Impact The order also allows a partial cut to steel and aluminum tariffs—but only for producers in Canada or Mexico supplying U.S. truck and car manufacturers. In the short term, the higher truck tariffs will likely raise vehicle, transport, and logistics costs, eventually pushing up prices for goods like food, fuel, and daily necessities. Longer term, it’s designed to push manufacturing back to America and reduce supply chain dependence on imports. 📩 Send us a message and let us know how we can help #ManufacturingJobs #TariffImpact #AmericanIndustry #TradePolicy #SupplyChainResilience #TruckManufacturing #USeconomy #DomesticProduction #EconomicGrowth
