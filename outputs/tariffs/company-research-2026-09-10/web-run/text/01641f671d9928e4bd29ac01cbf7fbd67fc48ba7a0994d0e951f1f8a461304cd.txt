      A Closed-Cycle with Profit: NUST MISIS Experts Calculate How Metallurgists Can Profit from Sludge Recycling Double Benefit: A Combination of Well-Known Technologies Will Allow Metallurgists to Profit from Waste and Reduce Environmental Impact Waste-Free Metallurgy: A New Approach Turns Environmental Costs into Profit To transition to a closed-loop economy in the metallurgical industry, NUST MISIS experts have proposed a concept for new waste recycling technology complexes. This new approach will ultimately allow companies to effectively incorporate sludge into production, saving additional resources and reducing environmental impact.Modern metallurgical plants are faced with increasing volumes of recyclable materials—dust, sludge, and similar materials that are currently considered waste. Their accumulation entails economic costs and environmental risks. Traditional recycling methods often fail to account for differences in chemical composition, limiting the incorporation of waste into production and leading to the loss of valuable components, primarily iron and zinc. To address this issue, NUST MISIS experts have improved blast and tube furnace-based process systems that effectively process waste and return it to the production cycle. This approach allows for the separation of material streams based on their composition: for example, zinc-rich dust is processed in a tube furnace using Waelz technology, while iron-rich clinker, sludge, and scale are processed in a mini-blast furnace."The proposed systems will allow companies to generate additional profits through waste recycling. According to our calculations, the investment in furnace upgrades will pay for itself within a few years, and then begin to generate a stable income. The implementation of such systems will provide a return on investment of up to 36%. Furthermore, the environmental impact is reduced, as millions of tons of dust and sludge no longer accumulate in waste dumps and are reused in production," explained Aitber Bizhanov, PhD, a leading expert in the NUST MISIS Department of Functional Nanosystems and High-Temperature Materials. The integrated use of different types of units increases processing efficiency through synergy: the quality of zinc sublimates is improved, iron recovery is increased, and fuel costs are reduced. With the proposed solutions, the recovery rate of both zinc and iron approaches 100%. Detailed research results are available in the scientific journal "Problems of Ferrous Metallurgy and Materials Science" (Q2)."A rational choice of processing technologies can significantly increase the use of secondary materials in production. In the future, the developed approaches will allow large metallurgical plants to improve economic efficiency while meeting modern environmental protection requirements," said Maria Ushakova, PhD in Engineering, Associate Professor in the Department of Business Informatics and Production Management Systems at NUST MISIS.
