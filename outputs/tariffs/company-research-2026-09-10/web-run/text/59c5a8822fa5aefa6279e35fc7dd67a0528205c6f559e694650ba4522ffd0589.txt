Understanding the New U.S. Heavy Truck Tariffs
A new 25% tariff on imported heavy trucks imposed by the U.S. government starting October 1, 2025, has caught the attention of major European truck manufacturers. This move, announced without detailed guidance, has sparked immediate reactions across stock markets, especially among companies that operate assembly plants in Mexico and rely on cross-border trade.
Stock Market Reactions and Company Positions
Daimler Truck Holding AG and Volkswagen AG’s Traton SE both saw their shares dip shortly after the tariff announcement. Daimler Truck’s shares fell as much as 4.9% in Frankfurt, while Traton’s dropped around 3.1%. Both companies have manufacturing footprints in Mexico, exposing them to potential tariff-related disruptions in their supply chains.
Meanwhile, shares of Sweden’s Volvo AB, known for producing Volvo and Mack trucks locally within the U.S., rose by about 3.45% in Stockholm’s market. Analysts suggest that Volvo's more domestic manufacturing approach, with a significant portion of their trucks produced in the U.S. rather than Mexico, may shield them somewhat from the brunt of tariffs.
Impact on Manufacturing and Supply Chains
Daimler Truck generates about 40% of its global sales in the U.S., where brands like Western Star, Thomas Built Buses, and Freightliner hold strong market positions—especially Freightliner, which dominates the heavy-duty segment. The company’s operations include plants in North Carolina and Oregon, but it also imports vehicles from Mexico. This cross-border dependence could lead to delays and increased costs due to tariff-related barriers.
Similarly, Traton has already experienced a decline in deliveries and orders in North America due to tariff uncertainties, even before the official announcement. It has reduced production shifts at its International Motors plant in Mexico, signaling caution about ongoing demand and investment decisions.
Volvo claims that 60-70% of the content of its U.S.-assembled trucks is locally sourced, although some parts still need to be imported. The company is awaiting official tariff legislation to assess the full impact.
Trade Barriers and Technological Investment
The truck tariffs come amid already rising trade barriers affecting the transport sector, like the 15% duties on cars imported from the European Union, hitting brands such as Porsche AG and Audi. European truck manufacturers have been pouring investment into advanced technologies like batteries and hydrogen fuel cells, aiming to stay competitive despite the turbulent trade environment.
