While the holiday period was fairly eventful from a trade perspective, it wasn’t quite drop what you’re doing and get back to work right now eventful. (Unless you export/import things from Venezuela.)
The only thing I ended up opining on was this story in The Times:
And, as long time readers of MFN will know, nothing gets me more excited than electric vehicle rules of origin.
At its core, the issue is simple: electric vehicles traded between the EU and UK currently qualify for tariff-free trade thanks to a rules of origin derogation, which allows them to use a higher proportion of foreign content and, crucially, foreign batteries.
After already being extended once, this derogation is set to expire at the end of 2026.
And this creates a problem. Because the EU and UK still aren’t making enough EV batteries.
Or, more precisely — and as I told The Times — they aren’t making enough active cathode material (CAM).
Britain has struggled to build the infrastructure required to produce EV batteries at a large scale. Sam Lowe, a trade expert at the Flint Global consultancy, said: “While everyone describes it as a battery sourcing issue, the main challenge is getting hold of locally sourced active cathode material. At the moment there isn’t enough supply in the EU or UK. Without it, most EVs will struggle to qualify for zero tariffs under the rules of origin changes.”
Why does this matter? Because of how the rule itself works.
From 1 January 2027, for an electric vehicle to qualify for tariff-free trade between the EU and UK:
- 55% of its ex-works value must originate in the EU and/or UK; and
- the battery pack must be EU/UK-originating.
For a battery pack to be considered EU/UK-originating, either:
- 70% of its value must be created in the EU and/or UK; or
- EU/UK manufacturing must result in a change in tariff heading — provided the active cathode material itself originates in the EU or UK.
Which means, de facto, that to qualify, battery packs must use EU/UK-originating CAM.
Rule change breakdown here:
The intention is clear: create a market for domestic EU/UK CAM and battery production.
But in practice, this doesn’t seem to have worked. At last count, there are only a couple of major CAM producers operating at automotive scale serving EV manufacturers in the EU — Umicore (Poland) and BASF/CATL (Germany). There simply isn’t enough originating CAM to go around, and what does exist is expensive.
Which brings us to the real issue, in my view: Chinese CAM is much cheaper.
Let’s do a (deliberately simplified) thought experiment.
EU supplier A
Makes car in the EU, uses EU battery/CAM
- Battery/CAM: £50
- Rest of vehicle: £50
- Tariff when exported to UK (10%): £0
- Final price: £100
EU supplier B
Makes car in the EU, uses Chinese battery/CAM
- Battery/CAM: £20
- Rest of vehicle: £50
- Tariff when exported to UK (10%): £7
- Final price: £77
Under this exaggerated scenario, the tariff threat doesn’t meaningfully change company behaviour, because sourcing from China is still far cheaper.
Even if you doubled the price of the Chinese battery/CAM, it would still be more cost-effective, in this example, to absorb the tariff.
Which tells us something important: the TCA tariff cliff, on its own, isn’t enough to create a domestic EU market for CAM and batteries.
Politically, I don’t expect much movement on this until later in the year. For now, the Commission is focused on finalising its definition of CAM — specifically, at what point in the production process cathode material becomes “active”, which has material implications for origin.
But I’m increasingly wondering whether any further extensions or accommodations ahead of the end-2026 deadline will be accompanied by measures designed to narrow the price gap.
Those could include:
- With apologies for being a broken record on this, joint EU/UK tariffs on Chinese batteries and/or CAM.
- More subsidies for EU/UK battery and CAM production.
- Industry commitments — voluntary or otherwise — of some sort to do more making of stuff in the EU.
Anyway, I’m sure we’ll return to this as the year goes on.
