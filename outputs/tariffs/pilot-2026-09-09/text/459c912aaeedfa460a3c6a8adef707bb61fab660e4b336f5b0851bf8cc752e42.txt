Imports of Flat Panel Display Modules are Not a Threat to U.S. National Security 
Flat panel display modules (HTS 8524) are devices that utilize the flow of electric 
current to emit light from fine pixels, thereby enabling various display functions. Flat panel 
display modules incorporate semiconductor components to regulate the flow of electric 
current, resulting in the structural integration of some semiconductor elements. However, the 
portion of semiconductors typically account for less than 20% of the overall composition of 
flat panel display modules, and the manufacturing process of modules is based on legacy 
node technologies that are clearly distinct from advanced semiconductor processes. 
Therefore, even if semiconductor processes are partly utilized in the production of flat panel 
display modules, these modules should not be regarded as "downstream products that contain 
semiconductors," nor is there a justified reason to regulate them as semiconductor derivatives 


[Page 2]
in the Section 232 investigation of semiconductors. Subjecting flat panel display modules to 
Section 232 measures would also be inconsistent with the objectives underlying the initiation 
of the Section 232 investigations into semiconductors and semiconductor manufacturing 
equipment. 
Flat panel display modules are primarily used as intermediate components in consumer 
products such as smartphones, tablets, televisions, and automotive infotainment systems. In 
particular, OLED flat panel display modules (HTS 8524.12/8524.92) are predominantly 
supplied for consumer applications. Given these factors, imports of flat panel display 
modules into the United States only contribute to the welfare of U.S. consumers and do not 
present a threat to U.S. national security. There is no meaningful connection between flat 
panel display modules imports and U.S. national security. 
Also, the U.S. International Trade Commission's U.S. Trade & Tariff Data clearly indicate 
that imports of flat panel display modules do not present a national security risk. In 
2024, according to such data, U.S. imports of flat panel display modules totaled 
approximately $1.7 billion, representing only 0.05% of total U.S. imports. Furthermore, 
the U.S. imported these modules from 53 countries, reflecting a diversified source of 
supply-flat panel display imports from Korea accounted for 13% ($200 million), following 
China (34%), Japan (24%), and Taiwan (14%). Given the relatively small share of total 
imports and the diversification of supply sources, there is no reasonable basis to conclude 
that imports of flat panel display modules adversely affect U.S. national security. 
The Korean Flat Panel Display Industry Contribute to the U.S. Supply Chain and 
Related Industries 
Korean flat panel display module manufacturers, including Samsung Display and LG 
Display, have established long-standing strategic partnerships with both upstream and 
downstream electronics industries in the United States. Korean display manufacturers remain 
committed to advancing technology and strengthening collaboration throughout the supply 
chain, thereby contributing to the growth and resilience of the U.S. electronics sector. 
In particular, Korean display manufacturers currently maintain close collaboration with 
various U.S.-based material, component, and equipment manufacturers, establishing a tightly 
integrated supply chain between Korea and the United States. U.S. companies, such as 
Universal Display Corporation, Coming, Dupont, and Applied Materials provide key 
materials and equipment essential to the production of flat panel display modules, thereby 
making substantial contributions to the production process.

Negative Impact of Tariff Measures on Flat Panel Display Modules 
Imposing tariffs on flat panel display modules would lead to increase input costs for U.S. 
downstream industries that import these modules to manufacture electronic products. This 
rise in production costs will eventually be passed on to U.S. consumers, resulting in higher 
prices for smartphones, tablets, televisions, and other electronic products, ultimately 
increasing the burden on consumers and resulting in a contraction in demand. 
While such tariff measures would pose immediate difficulties for Korean flat panel 
display module manufacturers, they would also negatively impact the U.S. upstream 
industry—including companies such as Universal Display Corporation, Corning, Dupont, 
and Applied Materials—involved in the supply chain, as well as the U.S. downstream 
industry—including companies such as Apple, GM, Ford, and Tesla—that utilize these 
modules to manufacture finished products. 
Therefore, any decision regarding the imposition of tariffs on flat panel display modules 
should be made through a comprehensive and measured assessment, taking into account the 
potential negative ripple effects across related U.S. industries, supply chains, and consumers.

Conclusion 
Flat panel display modules, while incorporating certain semiconductor elements—
typically less than 20%, are technically and functionally distinct from typical semiconductor 
products. These display modules are primarily used in civilian-use consumer goods such as 
smartphones, tablets, televisions, and automotive infotainment systems, directly contributing 
to U.S. consumer welfare. Accordingly, imports of flat panel display modules manufactured 
by Korean companies are not a threat to U.S. national security and should be excluded from 
the scope of any Section 232 measure. 
Even if there is a reasonable basis for regulating certain flat display modules 
used for non-civilian purposes, products that are predominantly supplied for consumer 
applications, such as OLED flat panel display modules (HTS 8524.12/8524.92), have 
no relevance to national security concerns and thus should not be subject to Section 232 
measures. 
Further, as explained above, the Korean flat panel display industry has established a robust 
supply chain through close cooperation with related U.S. upstream and downstream 
industries. Imposing tariff measures on flat panel display modules from Korean 
manufacturers would negatively impact the established supply chain and cooperation 
framework between the industries of both countries, potentially undermining the 
competitiveness of related U.S. sectors and burdening U.S. consumers. In light of these 
considerations, we respectfully request that, should the Department of Commerce decide 
to recommend any measures including tariffs against imports of flat panel display modules 
pursuant to the ongoing Section 232 investigation into semiconductor derivatives, imports of 
Korean flat panel display modules be excluded from the scope of such measures. 
Finally, as an ally of the United States for over seven decades, Korea will continue to 
strengthen partnership and cooperation with the United States. The Korean flat panel display 
industry will maintain its critical role in the U.S.-related supply chain and remain committed 
to ongoing cooperation to foster innovation and competitiveness in related U.S. industries. 
We sincerely hope that the Department of Commerce will give due weight to these 
considerations as it moves forward with its decision-making.
