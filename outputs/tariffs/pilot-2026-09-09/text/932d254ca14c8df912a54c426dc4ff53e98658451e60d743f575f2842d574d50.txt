Wire harnesses play a crucial role in modern technology, connecting electrical and electronic components in a wide array of applications, including automobiles, consumer electronics, industrial machinery, and aerospace systems. These intricate bundles of wires are essential for transmitting power and signal efficiently, making them a vital component in today's interconnected world. To facilitate global trade and harmonize product classification, the international community employs a standardized coding system known as the Harmonized System (HS) code. In this article, we will introduce and explore the HS code for wire harnesses, shedding light on its significance, and offering a comprehensive overview.
The Harmonized System is an internationally recognized classification system used to classify goods traded across borders. It was developed by the World Customs Organization (WCO) to standardize product classification and facilitate international trade. The HS code comprises a series of numerical digits, organized hierarchically, providing a unique code for each product category. This system is widely used by customs authorities, logistics providers, and businesses to identify and categorize goods accurately during import and export processes.
The HS code for wire harnesses falls under Chapter 85 of the Harmonized System, which covers electrical machinery and equipment, and parts thereof. More specifically, wire harnesses are classified under HS code 8544.30. This code represents "Insulated (including enameled or anodized) wire, cable (including coaxial cable), and other insulated electric conductors, whether or not fitted with connectors; optical fibre cables, made up of individually sheathed fibers, whether or not assembled with electric conductors or fitted with connectors."

Wire harnesses imported into the United States are subject to tariffs determined by their classification under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS). Most wire harnesses fall under HTS codes 8544.30.00 (vehicle-specific harnesses) or 8544.42.00 (general electrical harnesses), with standard duty rates ranging from 0% to 2.5%, depending on their application and design. However, additional tariffs may apply based on geopolitical factors. For example, imports originating from China are often subject to Section 301 tariffs, which can add 7.5% to 35% to the base rate, unless specific exclusions apply. Recent trade policies have maintained these tariffs, though businesses should monitor updates, as exemptions for certain products may periodically expire or be reinstated.
For precise rates, consult the USITC Tariff Database (hts.usitc.gov) or a licensed customs broker. Always verify the latest regulations, as trade policies and exemptions may change.
