### 🔩 Materials (Steel, Aluminum)

 Tariffs on raw materials are handled separately — often 25–50%
 Automotive-specific tariffs usually override metal tariffs to prevent double taxation (“destacking” rules) [...] It varies. While the base rate for many countries is still 25%, new trade deals have reduced rates for some — including 15% for the EU and Japan, and 10% for select UK vehicles. Other countries like China face 30% or higher.

### Are car parts also taxed under the 2025 tariffs?

Yes. A 25% tariff applies to most imported auto parts, unless they are USMCA-compliant or fall under a specific exemption. This affects engines, electronics, brake systems, and more. [...] Example: A Chinese brake rotor taxed at 25% steel tariff will not be taxed an additional 25% as an auto part

### 🛠️ Exceptions for Essential Replacement Parts

Select categories of parts used for maintenance and safety repairs may receive exemptions — especially:

 Brake systems
 Airbags
 Headlights and lighting modules

Exemptions are granted through a petition process and apply only to direct replacements for vehicles already on U.S. roads.
