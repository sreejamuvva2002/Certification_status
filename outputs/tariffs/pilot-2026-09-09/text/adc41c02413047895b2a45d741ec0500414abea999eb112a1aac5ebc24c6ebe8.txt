Start in the cell “Product or Country of Origin” (cell A8). Go down this column and stop when you reach a row that describes your imported product.
Example 1:
- Your product is a semiconductor or their derivative product.
- You go down the column and stop at the first row (cell A9).
Example 2:
- Your product is not a semiconductor or their derivative product.
- Your product is an auto/auto part.
- You go down the column and stop at the second row (cell A10).
Example 3:
- Your product is not a semiconductor or their derivative product.
- Your product is not an auto/auto part.
- Your product is a medium- or heavy-duty truck or part.
- You go down the column and stop at the third row (cell A11).
Once you reach the applicable row for your product, STOP. Use that row only and read from left to right. No other row will be used, even if the product in question has materials that may be subject to multiple different tariffs under Section 232 or IEEPA.
The column headings are the various Presidential Proclamations issued pursuant to Section 232 and Executive Orders issued pursuant to IEEPA that your product may be subject to (see the cell notes on the applicable Executive Order, Proclamation, and guidance CSMS messages).
As you read from left to right, you will be able to determine the tariff that your product is subject to.
- If the cell is gray, the tariff is not applicable to that product—keep reading to the right.
- If the cell says YES, or YES with a condition, then your product is either unconditionally (for cells marked YES without any condition) or conditionally (for cells marked YES with a condition) subject to that Proclamation or Executive Order. You will need to consult the specific Proclamation or Executive Order to determine if it provides for an exception that may be applicable to your product and, if not, then you will need to consult the implementing Annex for the tariff HTSUS provision and rate that applies.
- If the cell says NO, then that Proclamation or Executive Order does not apply. If a corresponding HTSUS classification must be declared to claim the non-applicability of that proclamation or Executive Order to your product, you will need to declare such classification with the entry summary (relevant HTSUS classifications are included in the “notes” for cells indicating NO).
Please note:
- If a cell says YES, that means that an Executive Order or Proclamation applies to the product, but it does not necessarily mean that additional duties apply because each Executive Order/Proclamation has exceptions that may apply. Thus, YES means that the specified Executive Order or Proclamation must be consulted to evaluate whether the tariffs or an exception provided by that particular instrument may apply to the specific product, whereas a NO means that the specified Executive Order or Proclamation does not apply (and hence its internal exceptions need not be evaluated).
  - For example, a “YES” in the “IEEPA Canada” column means that EO 14193 (as amended) applies, but that EO in turn includes exceptions (such as for USMCA-originating goods or informational materials), the application of which will need to be evaluated under the terms of that EO; thus the EO “applies” in the sense that such an evaluation of whether or not an internal exception applies is required, and depending on whether any of that EO’s internal exceptions apply, the product may or may not be assessed a tariff under that EO. In contrast, a “NO” in the “IEEPA Canada” column means that that EO does not apply in any event, so there is no need to evaluate whether any of its own internal exceptions are applicable.
- This chart is intended to assist with determining which Executive Orders and Proclamations may “stack”, i.e., apply concurrently to the same product. The chart does not determine exceptions for any particular importation.
