GARY WISENBAKER: Georgia ports weather tariff turbulence
Published 7:22 am Monday, November 3, 2025
Savannah rides a tariff-fueled surge while Brunswick’s auto trade hits the brakes. The Georgia Ports Authority is doing what it does best: moving cargo, even as President Donald Trump’s second term tariff storm rolls across the global trade horizon. The Port of Savannah’s container docks are humming, while Brunswick’s auto terminals are taking a hit. It’s a tale of two ports, one booming, one bracing.
The numbers tell the story. Savannah moved 5.7 million containers in fiscal 2025, up 8.6% from the year before — the second-busiest year in port history. Since 2016, its traffic has grown at a 4.5% annual clip, outpacing the national port average of 2.7%.
September alone saw 486,000 boxes, up 8% year over year. rail lifts jumped 21%, and trucks rolled through 316,000 gate transactions with a brisk 50-minute turnaround.
So much for tariffs killing trade. In Savannah, the opposite is happening.
The surge, ironically, is a side effect of the tariff threat itself. Importers, worried about cost hikes, rushed to get goods into the country before the new duties kicked in. The Atlanta Journal- Constitution reported that Savannah’s traffic “returned at near-record volumes” after a brief slowdown tied to Mr. Trump’s “Liberation Day” tariffs. The Associated Press added that retailers “scrambled to stock up,” creating an artificial wave of imports.
Nationwide, ports saw double-digit import jumps in early 2025. Savannah was one of the big winners. But these front-loaded shipments can’t last forever. GPA officials expect the pace to ease later this year once warehouses are full and the panic buying subsides.
Call it the tariff whipsaw: a short-term sugar rush for port numbers, followed by a likely slowdown when the adrenaline wears off.
Drive a hundred miles south, and the story changes. At Brunswick’s Colonel’s Island Terminal, September’s 55,811 auto and heavy-equipment units marked a steep 30% drop from last year. Big machines were the hardest hit, down more than 25%.
This is what tariffs look like on the ground. Manufacturers are holding back shipments from Europe, Asia, and Mexico as costs rise and markets shift. Brunswick’s total auto volume for FY2025 slipped 2.2%, and GPA chief Griff Lynch blamed the downturn squarely on auto-import tariffs.
So while container cargo is coasting on a tariff tailwind, the roll-on/roll-off side is squarely in the headwind.
The difference may lie in how well Georgia’s ports operate. Savannah’s efficiency numbers remain among the nation’s best: 32-minute truck turn times and 22-hour rail dwell averages. When shippers look for reliability, that kind of performance gets their attention.
GPA isn’t standing still either. It poured $470 million into upgrades last year and has another $472 million approved for projects stretching from Brunswick’s rail yards to Savannah’s Berth 4 and Ocean Terminal. Those investments are designed to capture freight diverted from less nimble ports — especially as global supply chains twist under political pressure.
For now, GPA is riding the wave. Container traffic is growing, capacity is expanding, and the state’s logistics reputation keeps climbing. But there are a few caution flags snapping in the breeze such as the surge may be temporary, driven by front-loading, pain in the auto sector, and the shifting ground endemic to trade negotiations.
From a Georgia economic standpoint, the outcome is still good news. Strong port performance supports thousands of jobs and keeps the state’s freight economy rolling. Some of this growth may be borrowed, however, from the future, pulled forward by a global game of tariff dodgeball.
The coming months will test whether this boom has staying power. A few key things to watch:
— Tariff clarity — If the White House backs off or trade deals stabilize, shipping may return to a steady, not sprinting, pace.
— Port competition — Savannah’s efficiency could attract even more business as shippers consolidate at high-performance gateways.
— Auto rebound — Brunswick’s fortunes depend on where automakers choose to build and ship next year.
— Infrastructure payoff — GPA’s investment bet needs to keep paying off in speed and reliability.
— Post-surge cooldown — Once the tariff rush passes, growth could flatten out fast.
Georgia’s ports are proving that preparation and performance can overcome politics — at least for a while. Tariffs have yet to sink Savannah; in fact, they’ve inflated its numbers. Brunswick, however, is feeling the opposite effect.
So, as 2025 winds down, the message is cautious optimism. Georgia is moving more freight, building more capacity, and keeping its logistics edge sharp, and proving that its world class management can withstand the tariff winds.
