At present, there is a 10% universal tariff on most goods imported to the U.S. However, individual nations and specific goods have also been targeted with their own specific tariffs based on the status of their trade relationship. Japanese cars, for instance, face a 25% tariff. At the moment, there are no U.S. tariffs on printed circuit board imports specifically. However, imported PCBs and their components and materials are still subjected to both the universal 10% tariff and the tariffs [...] As such, each business relying on PCBs will need to evaluate its own supply chain to determine the specific effect of tariffs on its import prices. Even if your business is not importing fully functional PCBs from China or Mexico, you may still have to deal with increased prices on copper, semiconductors and other PCB components. The import price may be lower overall, but it will still be increased by the universal 10% tariff at a bare minimum. [...] China — the largest producer of PCBs in the world — has been the primary target of the administration’s tariffs. As such, imported goods from China currently face the highest country-specific tariff rates of any nation in the world, with the average imported good facing a 30% tariff at the very least. However, even this lowered tariff rate may not last, as it is the result of a 90-day trade agreement between the U.S. and China.
