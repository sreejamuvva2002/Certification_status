Bare printed circuit boards have their own heading, 8534, which covers 'printed circuits'; boards with conductive tracks, but before electronic components are mounted. This is the home for the unpopulated PCB as manufactured.
The statistical breakouts within 8534.00.00 distinguish boards primarily by construction. Multilayer boards with three or more layers of conducting material are broken out separately from boards with fewer layers. Boards are also distinguished by substrate: those with a base wholly of impregnated paper (such as common paper-phenolic boards) versus other bases, and a specific line for boards with a ceramic base. Flexible printed circuits (FPCs) have their own breakout. All of these lines are duty-free, so the breakouts matter for statistical reporting rather than duty.
The most important classification point is the boundary between a bare board and a populated assembly. Heading 8534 covers the printed circuit itself. Once components (resistors, capacitors, integrated circuits and the like) are soldered onto the board to create a printed circuit assembly (PCBA), the populated board takes on the character of a part of a finished article and is generally classified as a part of the machine or apparatus it is destined for. For example, a populated board for a computer points toward heading 8473, and a populated board for telecommunications apparatus toward the parts provisions of heading 8517. Reviewing whether the board is bare or populated, and if populated what device it belongs to, is therefore the key step.
