The Classification Logic of Aluminum Castings in the HS Code System
straight answer: The primary basis for classifying aluminum castings under Chapter 76 (HS Code 7616.99) rather than Chapters 84 or 85 is General Rule 1 (GRI 1) of the Harmonized System—"Classification according to the provisions of the headings and the notes to the chapters." As long as the castings themselves have not been machined into "identifiable specific machine parts," they are classified under Chapter 76 based on their material. This is the most common point of error in customs audits.
Why not Chapters 84 and 85?
Note 2 to Chapter 15 clearly stipulates that general-purpose parts (such as bolts and springs) and castings or rough-machined products made solely of aluminum shall not be classified under Chapter 16 (Machinery and Equipment). Even if a die-cast blank is ultimately installed in an engine, as long as it is delivered in the form of a blank or has undergone only gate removal, it must be classified under 7616.99 rather than 8409 (engine parts).
The "most specific description takes precedence" principle under GRI 3(a)
- Return to Chapter 76: Unfinished aluminum castings whose specific use cannot be independently identified (accounting for approximately 70% of the actual declared quantity)
- Return to Chapter 84/85: Finished parts that have been precision-machined using CNC and are ready for specific mechanical functions (e.g., transmission housing 8708.40)
I encountered this issue in 2023 while assisting a die-casting factory in South China with a challenge raised by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP): The client had declared blanks for new energy motor end caps under heading 8503.00, but the entry was reclassified to 7616.99.5160, resulting in a total of $42,000The key factor in determining this is that—even after the goods arrive in the U.S., the customer must still drill and tap the holes themselves, so they do not qualify as "specialized parts."

Detailed Explanation of U.S. HTS Code 7616.99.5160
straight answer: HTS 7616.99.5160 is the 10-digit tariff code established by U.S. Customs for "other aluminum castings," with a base MFN rate of 2.5%However, with the additional tariffs imposed on China under Section 301, the actual comprehensive landed cost for aluminum castings originating in China is typically around 27.5%(approximately 2.51 TP3T + 251 TP3T). In addition, there may be an additional 101 TP3T surcharge on aluminum products under Section 232, as well as reciprocal tariffs imposed under certain new policies in 2025.
Code Breakdown and Scope of Application
- 7616: Other aluminum products
- 99: Items not listed
- 51: Castings (casting category; a classification specific to the United States)
- 60: Statistical suffix, used to distinguish specific purposes
Key difference: The EU CN code 7616.99.90 does not include a separate subheading for "Castings," and the Chinese Customs code 7616.9990 also does not specify the manufacturing process. In other words, for the same sand-cast aluminum bracket, when exported to the EU, it is classified under 7616.99.90, but when exported to the U.S., it must be classified under 7616.99.5160—process information is a mandatory declaration field for U.S. CBP.

Common Errors in the Classification of Aluminum Castings and Case Studies of Associated Risks
straight answer: In customs audits, misclassification of aluminum castings (70% and above) is primarily concentrated in three categories: incorrect classification of specialized parts under 7616.99, declaration as raw castings despite surface treatment, and confusion between castings and forgings. Incorrect declarations not only require the payment of back taxes but also incur administrative fines of 30% to 2 times the declared value, as well as a late filing penalty of 0.05% per day.
Three Common Types of Errors and the Risk of Having to Pay Back Taxes
- Error 1: Specialized parts were incorrectly classified under the general code 7616.99.The most common examples are castings with a "specialized" nature, such as automotive transmission housings and engine mounts, which traders often declare under 7616.9990 for the sake of convenience. I handled a case in 2023: A Ningbo-based exporter declared transmission housings under HS code 7616.99 with a declared value of $5 million. Customs determined that they should be classified under HS code 8708.40 (a tariff rate difference of approximately 41% vs. 3%), resulting in a total of $280,000 in back taxes and late payment penalties.
- Mistake 2: Declaring the goods as raw materials even after surface treatment.Once castings have undergone anodizing, powder coating, or CNC machining, they are no longer considered "castings not further worked." According to the notes in the WCO HS Nomenclature 2022, the degree of processing determines the classification level.
- Mistake 3: Using the same code for castings and forgings.Forged aluminum parts should be classified under the various subheadings of 7616.99; their microstructure is entirely different from that of castings, which can be readily verified through a customs inspection of the shipment in conjunction with a metallographic report.
Risk Mitigation Recommendations
It is recommended that exporters request an End-Use Statement from their customers at the contract stage and retain the casting process flowchart for five years for future reference. For companies with annual export volumes exceeding $10 million, the inspection rate can be reduced to below 0.51% after applying for AEO certification from customs. Frequently Asked Questions
Do aluminum die-castings and sand-cast aluminum castings have the same HS code?
The same. Both are classified under 7616.99 (U.S. HTS 7616.99.5160). The HS code classifies items based on material and form, without distinguishing between die casting, sand casting, or gravity casting processes. However, the type of casting process must be accurately stated in the declaration details, as customs will use this information to verify the reasonableness of the unit price—the unit price for die-cast parts is typically 15–30% higher than that for sand-cast parts.
How are unmachined castings and finished castings classified?
The key factor is whether the item constitutes a "distinctly identifiable specific part." Raw parts that have only had their gates removed, been sandblasted, or deburred remain classified under 7616.99; Finished parts that have undergone CNC finishing, meet assembly tolerances, and are specifically intended for a particular machine should be classified under the corresponding machinery chapter (e.g., engine blocks under 8409). The criteria for determination refer to General Rule 2(a) of the WCO’s HS Classification Manual.
What supporting documents are required for exporting aluminum castings?
- Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading
- Material Test Certificate (MTC, including alloy designations such as ADC12 and A356)
- Certificate of Origin (FORM E/RCEP, eligible for preferential tariff rates)
- Exports to the U.S. require ISF 10+2 filing
