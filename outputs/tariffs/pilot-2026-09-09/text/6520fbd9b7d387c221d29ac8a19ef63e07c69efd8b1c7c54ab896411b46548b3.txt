What Changed for Auto Parts from Taiwan

Under the U.S.–Taiwan trade framework announced on January 15, tariffs on goods imported from Taiwan, including automotive parts, will be capped at no more than 15 percent. This includes any duties applied under Section 232, the national security authority that has previously been used to impose tariffs on auto parts. [...] For ABPA members, this is an important clarification. While tariffs are not being eliminated, the agreement provides a clear upper limit and avoids the risk of higher or more punitive duty rates that had been a concern in recent trade discussions. The tariff level brings Taiwan more in line with other major U.S. trading partners in the Asia-Pacific region and helps maintain Taiwan as a competitive and reliable source of automotive components.

Why Semiconductors Are Getting the Headlines [...] Much of the public attention around this announcement is centered on semiconductors, driven by large investment commitments by Taiwanese chip manufacturers and U.S. efforts to expand domestic semiconductor production. These provisions include incentives and tariff measures that are largely unrelated to automotive parts.
