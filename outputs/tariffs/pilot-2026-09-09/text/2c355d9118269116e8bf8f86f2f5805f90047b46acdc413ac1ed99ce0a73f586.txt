Customs Valuation [Royalties] in Contract Manufacturing – Key Takeaways A recent India CESTAT ruling (Customs Appeal No. 40085/2024) provides important clarity on when royalties paid by a beneficial owner become dutiable, even if the importer of record is a contract manufacturer. What happened? Xiaomi India paid royalties/licence fees to Qualcomm and Beijing Xiaomi for technology embedded in imported components and finished goods. While many goods were manufactured by Indian contract manufacturers, Xiaomi China controlled the import of components and the manufacturers could only sell their output to Xiaomi India. Revenue argued—and CESTAT agreed—that: The IP was embedded in the imported goods. Non-payment of royalties would halt imports entirely. Royalties were therefore a condition of sale and must be included in the customs assessable value. Xiaomi India, though not the importer of record, acted as the beneficial owner and was liable for valuation impacts. Why it matters: Royalty payments linked to the right to import can trigger customs duty—even if paid by a party other than the importer. Beneficial ownership extends beyond legal title to include control, benefit, and contractual influence. Contract manufacturing and IP licensing structures must be carefully reviewed for customs implications. An important reminder for companies using contract manufacturers and relying on third-party IP: valuation risk doesn’t end with the importer of record—it follows the economic reality of the transaction. As this is a recent ruling, it carries significant industry implications—and we can expect the matter to be appealed to the Supreme Court. https://lnkd.in/e8GUAXBr
