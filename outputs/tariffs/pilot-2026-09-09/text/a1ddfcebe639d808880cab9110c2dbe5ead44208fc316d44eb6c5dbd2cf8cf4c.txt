For example, U.S. automakers import many battery materials from South Korea, via suppliers like SK On and LG Chem, which face the global 10 percent tariff — and the possibility of a far higher 25 percent duty after Trump’s reciprocal tariffs resume in July.

Then there’s finished vehicles that are made in Mexico and Canada, where Detroit’s automakers feel a particular pinch. [...] The tariffs are threatening to raise the costs of minerals used to build equipment like lithium-ion batteries and other equipment coming into the U.S. The materials from China that matter most are battery cells; cathode-active materials; and raw inputs like graphite, lithium, cobalt and nickel. [...] While cars of all kinds could get costlier with tariffs, those that run on batteries face more price challenges. Tariffs are highest on China, which automakers and suppliers can’t yet escape as a key source of crucial battery materials. Trade war developments “have put EV manufacturers in a uniquely difficult position,” said Ram Chandrasekaran, an EV analyst at FTI Consulting. “Many will struggle to offset these pressures without eroding already thin margins.”
