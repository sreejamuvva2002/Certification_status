Why Sodium-ion is a Smart Export Move
As global demand for energy storage accelerates, manufacturers face a critical choice: continue paying soaring tariffs on lithium-ion batteries—or explore a smarter, more cost-effective export path with sodium-ion batteries.
While lithium-ion batteries are increasingly burdened by high tariffs, anti-dumping duties, and tight import restrictions, sodium-ion batteries currently avoid most of these trade barriers.
United States: Sodium-ion 38.4% vs Lithium-ion 100%+
In the United States, lithium-ion batteries face a combined import tariff exceeding 100%, factoring in base duties, anti-dumping measures, and Section 301 tariffs.
In contrast, sodium-ion batteries are currently subject to a total import duty of just 38.4%, offering a significant cost advantage for exporters.
Takeaway: Sodium-ion batteries are taxed at nearly one-third the rate of lithium-ion batteries in the U.S.
European Union: 0% Tariff for Sodium-ion, High Duties for Lithium
The European Union has imposed a range of trade restrictions on Chinese lithium-ion batteries, including:
- 
Anti-dumping and countervailing duties
- 
Traceability and sustainability requirements
- 
Customs inspections and documentation burdens
By contrast, sodium-ion batteries currently enjoy zero tariff rates in the EU, with no anti-dumping actions in place.
Takeaway: Exporting sodium-ion to the EU is currently tariff-free and faces fewer policy hurdles.
Asia: Minimal or No Import Barriers
Major Asian markets—including India, South Korea, and Southeast Asia—have not imposed significant tariffs on sodium-ion batteries. Most countries maintain zero to low import duties on sodium-ion products.
Meanwhile, lithium batteries often face increased scrutiny or regulatory control in these regions.
Takeaway: Sodium-ion batteries are welcomed across Asia with low-to-zero import duties.
