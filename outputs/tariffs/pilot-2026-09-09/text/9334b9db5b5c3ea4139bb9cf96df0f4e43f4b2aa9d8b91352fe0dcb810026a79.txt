I. Adjustments to U.S. Tariff Policies: The Underlying Logic from "Exemption" to "Delayed Imposition"
1. Evolution of Tariff Policies
- Background in 2024: During the review of the "Section 301 Tariffs," the U.S. announced a 25% tariff on natural graphite, but postponed its implementation until 2026.
- New Developments in 2025: The White House released a tariff exemption list, excluding graphite from the 34% "reciprocal tariffs," highlighting the vulnerability of its supply chain.
          
        
2. Roots of the U.S.'s "Contradictory Actions"
- Dependency Dilemma: The U.S. relies on China for 80% of its graphite imports, particularly for high-purity graphite used in anode materials for new energy vehicle batteries.
- Strategic Delay: Postponing the tariff imposition is a stopgap measure aimed at buying time for the development of domestic supply chains. The U.S. plans to subsidize domestic graphite processing through the Inflation Reduction Act, but technological bottlenecks are difficult to overcome in the short term.
          
        
II. Direct Impacts of Tariffs on China's Natural Graphite Exports
1. Short-Term Pressures are Manageable, but Long-Term Risks Loom
- Cost Impact: If the 25% tariff is implemented, it will directly increase the price of Chinese graphite in the U.S. market, weakening its price competitiveness. It is estimated that the cost of natural graphite in the U.S. market could increase by 20%-30%, potentially causing some customers to switch to alternative suppliers in Africa, Brazil, etc.
- Industrial Chain Linkage Effects: Graphite is a core material for lithium battery anodes, and tariffs may be passed on to downstream EV companies, intensifying the Sino-U.S. "green technology war."
          
        
2. Potential Loss of Market Share
- In 2023, the U.S. accounted for 20.8% of China's lithium battery exports. If graphite costs rise, U.S. automakers may be forced to turn to South Korean and Japanese suppliers, indirectly squeezing China's market share.
          
        
III. China's Response Strategies: From "Passive Defense" to "Proactive Breakthrough"

1. Strengthening Supply Chain Control
- Export Control Countermeasures: In 2023, China included high-purity synthetic graphite and natural flake graphite in its export control list, using a licensing system to gain pricing power.
- Building Technological Barriers: Increasing the proportion of high value-added graphite products (such as spherical graphite and silicon-carbon composites) to consolidate dominance in the global high-end market.
          
        
2. Entrepôt Trade and Capacity Relocation
- Southeast Asia Layout: Using third countries like Malaysia and Thailand for entrepôt trade to circumvent U.S. tariffs. Some companies have set up processing bases in Southeast Asia to complete simple packaging before exporting to the U.S.
- Nearshoring Outsourcing: Drawing on the models of BYD and CATL, establishing factories in Mexico and Eastern Europe to leverage regional trade agreements (such as the USMCA) to reduce tariff impacts.
          
        
3. Diversifying Market Expansion
- European Alternative Markets: The EU plans to account for 25% of global battery production capacity by 2030. Chinese graphite companies can leverage this by partnering with major battery exporters like CATL and SVOLT.
- Penetrating Emerging Sectors: Growing demand for graphite in emerging industries such as hydrogen energy and nuclear energy opens up incremental markets to hedge risks.
IV. Future Outlook: Reshaping the Global Graphite Landscape Amid Sino-U.S. Rivalry
1. Limitations of the U.S.'s "De-Chinafication" Efforts
- The U.S. has low-grade domestic graphite mines, high environmental costs, and purification technology that lags behind China by over a decade, making self-sufficiency difficult to achieve in the short term. China has deepened cooperation with graphite-rich countries like Mozambique and Madagascar through the "Belt and Road" Initiative to control upstream minerals.
          
        
2. Upgrade Opportunities for China's Industrial Chain
- Technological Upgrades: Developing continuous graphitization and isostatic pressing technologies to reduce energy consumption and improve product consistency.
- Policy Dividends: China's promotion of the "equipment replacement" policy stimulates the domestic energy storage and new energy vehicle markets, absorbing excess capacity.
Conclusion
U.S. tariff policies present both challenges and opportunities. China's natural graphite industry must consolidate its core competitiveness through technological upgrades, address geopolitical risks with supply chain resilience, and leverage the global energy transition to promote "Chinese standards" worldwide.
