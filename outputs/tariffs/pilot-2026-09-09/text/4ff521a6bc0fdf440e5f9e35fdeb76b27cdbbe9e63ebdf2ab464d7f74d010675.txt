MCA appreciates this opportunity to comment on the proposed Section 301 tariffs. 
While MCA understands and supports the motivation for fairer trade policies and a level playing 
field, it respectfully requests that imports from China of certain lithium salts, certain carbonate 
solvents, and electrolyte additives for LIB production classified under HTS subheadings 
2826.90 .90, 2920 .90.51, and 2932.99.90 be removed from the list of products targeted for tariff 
treatment. Regardless of the level of tariffs assessed , MCA has no choice but to continue to 
purchase these products from China because no viable alternative sources of supply exist - either 
in the United States or in third countries - to replace what is currently sourced from China.

Testimony of Mr. Dennis Trice, President, Mitsubishi Chemical America, Inc. 
Good morning Mr. Chairman and members of the Committee. I am Dennis Trice, 
President of Mitsubishi Chemical America. Inc. Together with Japan ·s Mitsubishi Chemical 
Corporation and our twenty one U.S. subsidiaries, MCA is a global leader in the production of 
advanced chemicals for consumer products, medical and industrial applications, transportation, 
construction, and countless other industries. 
Our company has been and continues to be committed to growing our business in the 
Americas as evidenced by investments totaling nearly $900 million over the past 10 years. We 
employ over 2,600 skilled American workers and our U.S. operations contribute billions of 
dollars every year to the U.S. economy. 
Among our most significant recent investments was a new $38 million state-of-the-art 
manufacturing facility in Tennessee.just outside Memphis. Our subsidiary MC Ionic Solutions 
US, Inc. operates this Tennessee facility which accounts for virtually all production of 
formulated electrolyte for lithium ion batteries that will power American-made electric vehicles 
like the Tesla Model 3, Nissan Leaf and Chevy Bolt, and business is booming. 
Our investments in the Memphis factory have already created 60 new full-time 
manufacturing jobs with an average wage of nearly $65,000. Our current plan is to double 
production at the Tennessee plant by the end of next year, and to continue to hire more workers 
to meet strong demand. 

[Page 18]
Among the key inputs for the electrolyte made in Memphis are certain specialty 
chemicals, including certain Lithium salt, certain carbonate esters, and electrolyte additives. 
Currently, we mainly import these products from China under HTS subheadings 2826.90.90 and 
2920.90.51. 
These imports are absolutely critical to the success of our investment in Memphis and our 
ability to domestically manufacture the components for lithium ion batteries. These are the 
chemicals that make lithium ion battery production in the United States possible at plants like the 
Nissan factory in Smyrna, Tennessee, the LG Chem factory in Holland, Michigan, and the 
Panasonic factory in Reno, Nevada. 
It would be disastrous for us and our U.S. customers if USTR were to impose additional 
tariffs on them as a result of this Section 301 proceeding. There simply is no other viable 
source for these key inputs outside China in the volumes and at the quality levels that we require. 
Developing new sources outside of China to replace existing production would take massive, 
long~term investments and many years. Even then, China is likely to remain the sole viable 
source for many of these inputs and their component chemicals. 
Thus, if Section 301 tariffs are imposed, we will have no choice but to pay them. And 
the cost of even the 10% tariffs would be so high that it will force us to reconsider our 
investments in the United States and our plans to continue to grow our manufacturing footprint 
in this country. 
Worse yet, the tariffs will hit not only our U.S. operations, but those of downstream U.S. 
industries such as the lithium ion battery industry. the electric vehicle industry, and the tens of 
thousands of Americans employed by these industries. The tariffs on these critical components 
of lithium ion batteries will greatly undercut the efforts of U.S. automakers and other U.S. 
2 

[Page 19]
industries to remain competitive and develop new, energy efficient and high performance 
products. The U.S. government should support the growth of this fledgling industry 
domestically, not create incentives to move it overseas. 
At the same time, the tariffs will be unlikely to address the Chinese government policies 
discussed in USTR's Section 301 report. For years we have been able to source these materials 
from China without encountering intellectual property rights infringement or forced technology 
transfers. 
On behalf of MCA and its over 2,600 U.S. employees, as well as the whole of this new 
emerging industry, we deeply appreciate this opportunity to present the facts. While we 
understand and appreciate the policy goals of this Section 301 action to provide a level playing 
field for U.S. companies and all Americans, we respectfully submit that the proposed tariffs 
would have precisely the opposite of their intended effect if applied to imports of these specialty 
chemicals. Accordingly, USTR should remove them from the proposed Section 30 l tariff list. 
Thank you for your attention and I welcome any questions that you may have.
