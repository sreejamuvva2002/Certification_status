The auto retail industry is in a confusing place. On the one hand, 
auto sales are up and profits are strong. On the other hand, 
newly imposed tariffs have spooked many OEMs and consumer 
confidence has plunged to near record lows. There is some 
general faith that “things will work out – this is just the way Trump 
negotiates,” but it seems likely there will be some lingering tariffs 
that will cause pain to many for some time.
At the moment, auto dealers are feeling pretty confident. They have 
seen the auto retail business model endure all kinds of shocks and 
emerge highly profitable. Our friends tell us that they believe there 
will be a period of a few months to a year when the tariffs bite and 
profits may suffer. And then they believe there will be an extended 
period of economic growth that will lift consumer confidence, drive 
sales back above 17 million units, and boost dealership profitability.
In terms of the buy-sell market, things are also a little confused. We 
have never seen more demand for dealerships, as almost every 
day we are contacted by an existing dealer, a want-to-be dealer 
or an investor expressing their interest in buying dealerships. But 
so far in 2025, the number of dealerships sold has plunged by 57% 
from Q1 2024. Perhaps buyers and sellers were on the sidelines 
during the presidential election that went on during Q4 last year.
As for our own practice, we advised on the sale of 54 dealerships 
in 2024. Several of these dealerships sold for record high prices for 
their franchises as earnings remained strong. But we also began 
to see some dealerships falling into loss positions, leading to their 
owners choosing to sell them for just a few million dollars in blue 
sky.
As we turn to 2025, our list of client engagements has never been 
stronger. We have closed on the sale of 18 dealerships, have 
another 36 dealerships pending closing and are in-market with 
another 16 dealerships. Only one of these transactions has been 
impacted by tariffs, with a buyer for an Audi dealership asking for 
a pause until they can assess the impact on that brand. And the 
pricing for almost every transaction has been strong. We have 
seen no diminution of dealership values so far from tariffs. In our Q4 
2024 report, we made eight changes to blue sky multiples to reflect 
changing pricing for dealerships. In this report, we make none.
We believe that one reason auto dealers are calm is because 
they are making investments based upon a very long timeline 
– 20 years or more. They are less concerned about short-term 
economic issues, and more concerned with choosing the right 
franchises, retaining the best talent and treating their customers 
well. If they perform in the areas that they can control, dealers are 
confident the next few decades in auto retail will be as successful 
as the past few.  
Dealers, consumers and OEMs rejoice! On May 22nd, the Senate voted to revoke California’s emissions waivers, 
putting an end to the “California EV Mandate”, at least for now. California, along with 16 other states and the District of 
Columbia, have been pulled from the edge of a legislative cliff that would have likely resulted in a dramatic reduction 
of new vehicle sales in affected markets. While we agree wholeheartedly with California’s pursuit of cleaner air and a 
cooler climate, we believe there are better ways to pursue them than this legislation. The automotive industry wants 
to sell what consumers want.   And to that end, we believe the Senate made the correct decision, and one that will 
continue allowing American families freedom of choice while simultaneously protecting critical American jobs.

Auto dealership buy-sell activity 
plummeted in the first quarter of 
2025. According to our research 
and data from Automotive News, 
68 rooftops traded hands in the 
first quarter of 2025, a 57.0% decline from the first quarter last year. 
The Q1 slowdown arrived in stark contrast to 2024, which saw 563 
dealerships sold – making it the fourth-busiest year for dealership 
buy-sells. Q1 2025 was the slowest first quarter for dealership M&A 
since 2015. We are surprised by this decline since the market feels 
very active to us. When we query leading buyers and the buy-sell 
attorneys to get their opinions about the slowdown, they respond 
that they believe the presidential election that went on in the 4th 
quarter of last year is a potential explanation. Buyers wanted to 
know what kind of regulatory environment they were going to 
be operating in before handing over big amounts to purchase 
dealerships. And perhaps some potential sellers wanted to wait 
for more certainty about the future, with the hopes that their 
dealerships would be worth more if President Trump were re-
elected. As a result, few buy-sells were put together in the fourth 
quarter of 2024 that would have closed in the first quarter of 2025.
Of the 68 dealerships sold in Q1, 64 were acquired by private 
dealers. This ~90% ratio of private/public activity is about the same 
as what we have seen in recent years.  Lithia and AutoNation were

Public Company Acquisitions Slowed to a Near Standstill in Q1
The publicly traded auto retail companies had a very slow first 
quarter in terms of acquisitions. These consolidators, including 
Asbury, AutoNation, Group 1, Lithia, Penske, and Sonic, spent just 
$174M on acquisitions in Q1 2025, a 91.1% decrease compared to 
what the publics spent in Q1 last year.  
In terms of domestic auto dealership acquisitions in Q1 2025, 
AutoNation acquired Mazda and Ford dealerships in Colorado and 
Lithia acquired Subaru dealerships in Virginia and California.  
And as we stated earlier, Asbury expects to close the 33-store Herb 
Chambers deal in Q2 and Group 1 has acquired a Mercedes-Benz 
dealership in TX for amounts which will be far more than what the 
publics spent in the first half of 2024.
In a continued trend, U.S. auto retail companies are expanding 
overseas to continue building value for shareholders.  In the first 
quarter, Group 1 acquired four dealerships in the U.K., bringing 
their total up to 116 dealerships across the pond. Over the past 
year, Group 1 has spent $655M on international acquisitions and 
$0 on domestic U.S. dealership acquisitions.  But in their Q1 2025 
earnings call, Group 1, along with all their publicly traded auto 
retail counterparts, stated that they are continuing to monitor the 
marketplace for desirable acquisition opportunities.

Haig Partners National Average Blue Sky Multiples
We carefully monitor the buy-sell market to assess the desirability 
of various franchises and overall market conditions. We analyze 
offers for transactions we are involved in and regularly speak with  
leading dealership buyers as well as attorneys, bankers and CPAs 
involved in acquisitions.  
Most buyers base their blue sky offers on a multiple of the expected 
future cash flows. The amount of goodwill they are willing to pay is 
based upon what their targets are for return on investment, internal 
rate of return, payback period, or other valuation methodologies 
buyers choose. In general, franchises that provide high profits, 
growth and safety bring the highest multiples. Franchises that are 
declining, are “over-dealered” or are perceived as risky bring the 
lowest multiples.
After making a record eight changes to our National Average Blue 
Sky Multiples in our last report, we did not find sufficient evidence 
to merit any changes to multiples in the first quarter of 2025. If 
tariffs hold, they will likely have a significant impact on dealership 
profits, and therefore the prices that buyers will be willing to pay for 
acquisitions moving forward. As the impact of these new tariffs is 
felt over time, the franchise multiples published in this document 
will continue to reflect the pricing trends that we observe in the 
marketplace.  
The multiples set forth in the chart below reflect the pricing that 
we believe the market will yield for various franchises that perform 
at average levels during a competitive sales process. The actual 
blue sky multiple paid by a buyer can vary significantly from these 
levels for reasons we have set forth below.

Blue Sky Values Nearly Unchanged from 2024
The average publicly owned auto dealership in the U.S. had an estimated blue sky value of $20.7M in Q1 2025, a 1.0% decrease from 2024.  
At this level, the blue sky value for an average dealership is more than twice as much as it was in 2019, the year before the pandemic. Blue 
sky values are set by buyers and depend on their expectation of future profits. Profits and blue sky values peaked in 2023 and have been 
declining since then, but the rate of decline in dealership earnings slowed significantly in Q1, alleviating negative pressure on blue sky. 
It is not clear what will happen with dealership profits over the next three quarters. We don’t yet know if OEMs will absorb the higher costs, or if 
they will be paid by the customer or the dealer, or some combination of all three. It’s also possible the Trump administration will cut a “deal” 
with global automakers that will soften near term tariffs in return for long-term commitments to manufacture more in the US. If the current 
tariffs remain in place, some customers will no doubt push back on paying more for vehicles and parts. But there is still significant pent-up 
demand as sales have been well below historical averages for six years now. In addition, economists are still predicting two to three interest 
rate cuts between now and year end. And customers may choose to repair their vehicles instead of buying new ones, which will further boost 
fixed operations. For these reasons, dealership profits could remain level or even grow from what we are enjoying today. 
Despite the noise in today’s environment, many buyers feel confident that dealerships will continue to generate strong profits long into the 
future. For this reason, buyers today offering on average performing dealerships are typically using a multiple of current earnings rather than 
factoring in any decline in earnings. Buyers believe the impacts of the tariffs will be short lived, so if the right dealerships come along in a 
market that they like, they are making strong offers. To have a confidential discussion about what your dealership or dealership group could 
be worth, please reach out to any member of our team. We frequently provide no-cost consultations to dealers considering acquiring or 
divesting stores.

The core Personal Consumption Price Index (“PCE”), which the Fed 
utilizes as its preferred measure of inflation, rose 2.6% year-over-
year in March, nearing the Fed’s long-term inflation target of 2.0%.  
But on a month-over-month basis, core PCE, which eliminates 
highly variable costs like food and energy, was nearly unchanged.  
March marked the lowest month-over-month core PCE increase in 
nearly five years, suggesting a slowdown in inflation.
We were wondering if the auto industry would have been a cause 
for inflation in Q1, as some customers rushed out to purchase 
vehicles before the impact of tariffs made its way into MSRP.  
According to the March PCE report, consumers spent 56.5% more 
on motor vehicles & parts than they did in March 2024, claiming the 
greatest year-over-year spending increase across any vertical. 
Thankfully, the vehicles they purchased must have been about the 
same prices as those purchased last year.
Inflation Slows, Spending Accelerates
The situation with tariffs is fluid, with negotiations going on 
constantly between OEMs, foreign governments and the US 
government. Almost every week we learn of changes to the 
previous tariff regulations. The good news is that the level of tariffs 
appears to be going down, in some cases significantly. Prices 
will still likely be higher than they would have been otherwise, but 
OEMs have already begun to adjust their sourcing, production and 
model mix to compensate. Many dealers we speak to believe that 
tariffs will not be a catastrophe, but yet another challenge they will 
have to navigate.
Tariffs
There are a lot of mixed messages in the current economic data. Analysts must be throwing up their hands trying to give 
guidance to investors about the direction of our economy and whether to buy or sell, invest or divest, or just sit on the 
sidelines and wait until conditions settle.
During its May meeting, the Fed decided to maintain the previous 
target rate of 4.25-4.50%, despite increasing pressure from the 
current administration. The Fed decided to hold rates at this 
elevated level for two main reasons: inflation remains above the 
Fed’s 2% target, and the economic outlook is abnormally cloudy, 
with tariffs being a likely source of near term inflation.  
The Federal Reserve has two primary objectives, called a “dual 
mandate”. The Fed’s dual mandate states that the bank must both 
keep prices stable and maximize employment. And right now, the 
high level of economic uncertainty is making it challenging for 
the bank to assess whether it should focus more on supporting 
employment or constraining inflation. According to Fed chair 
Jerome Powell, “We’ve judged that the risk to higher inflation and 
unemployment has risen,” pointing to recent drops in consumer 
and business sentiment. But Mr. Powell had to caveat this statement 
by stating that these shifts in sentiment had not yet translated to 
hard economic data. Due to this uncertainty, we expect that the 
Fed will react slowly and cautiously this year, with fewer and later 
rate cuts than what was anticipated in late 2024.
Interest Rates Held in Place Due to 
Economic Uncertainty
With all of the economic noise in Q1, there was an abnormal amount 
of focus on the April jobs report. Economists and policymakers were 
worried that the disruptions caused by President Trump’s tariffs 
would lead U.S. employers to invest less, leading to a slowdown in 
the labor market. But fortunately, April’s jobs report landed with a 
bang!  In April, the U.S. economy added 177,000 jobs, far exceeding 
the projected gain of 135,000. Barring any future downward 
revisions, April’s gains were stronger than the average rate of 
gains observed throughout Q1, a sign of resilience during a period 
in which employers were under an abnormal level of economic 
uncertainty. The unemployment rate was 4.2% in April, a low level 
unchanged from March and in-line with expectations.

The consumer confidence index fell for the fourth straight month in April, plummeting 32.4% year-over-year to 52.2 out of a possible 100.  In 
April, sentiment fell unanimously across different age groups, geographies, income levels and political affiliations. According to the University 
of Michigan, which has tracked consumer confidence for decades, consumers in April reported warning signs that increase the risk of a 
recession, including deteriorating expectations for business conditions, personal finances, incomes, inflation and labor markets. Consumers 
also expect unemployment to rise significantly throughout the remainder of the year. Preliminary numbers for May are even worse, with 
the consumer confidence index trending towards 50.8 out of 100, down 3% from April, the second lowest on record. Year-ahead inflation 
expectations also surged from 5.0% in March to 6.7% in April, the highest reading since 1981. Consumers are clearly worried about the current 
direction of our economy.

SAAR Soars in Early 2025
Sales volumes shot up in Q1 2025, with March and April’s SAAR both topping the 17.0M mark. The SAAR for the first four months of 2025 was 16.7M, 
7.1% higher than the same period of 2024. According to Wards Intelligence, the SAAR in March and April would have been near 16.0M had it not 
been for a tariff-fueled shopping spree during those two months.
Inventories on the ground and in-transit experienced a year-over-year decline in April, the first in three years. If this sales rate continues, 
inventories are expected to decline further particularly as OEMs consider slowing production while the tariff situation is fluid. Due to widespread 
uncertainty around President Trump’s tariffs, many automotive retail thought leaders like NADA and Cox Automotive have downgraded their 
SAAR projections for 2025. NADA, for example, downgraded their 2025 SAAR projection from 16.2M in December to 15.0-16.0M in April 2025.

Inventory levels remained relatively stable to start the year but declined sharply at the end of March, 
driven by a sales surge of buyers rushing to purchase new vehicles ahead of the anticipated tariff 
increases.  In early April, the average new-vehicle day’s supply at U.S. dealerships had fallen to 70 days - 
down 21 days from the beginning of March and 10 days lower than the same time last year. Current 
inventory is at the lowest levels seen since 2023. According to Cox Automotive, six brands now report 
inventory levels below a 60-day supply, with Lexus and Toyota maintaining the lowest levels among brands we track, followed closely by 
Honda, Subaru, Porsche and BMW. On the other end of the scale, Jeep, Ram and Dodge, have the highest new vehicle supply among major 
franchises – all exceeding 100 days. Looking ahead, if auto tariffs do remain, we expect consumers may gravitate towards the brands that 
have more units in stock and are still for sale at pre-tariff prices. Consumers looking for a Lexus or a Toyota may be the first to feel price 
increases, assuming Toyota passes through the cost of tariffs to customers.

Most Brands Experienced Sales Gains in Q1
Q1 was a strong quarter for U.S. sales, with March reporting the highest monthly sales volume recorded in four years. But its results may be 
difficult to replicate later in 2025.  Brands leading first quarter gains include Porsche (+40.6%), JLR (+25.9%), followed closely by GM brands 
including Buick/GMC (+23.3%), Cadillac (+17.8%) and Chevrolet (+14.2%).  
In a show of strength, GM experienced segment-leading gains without the promotional pricing offered by rivals Ford and Stellantis in Q1, both 
of which experienced sales declines during the period. Affordable models like Chevrolet’s Trax (+57.0%) and Nissan’s Versa (+156.0%) excelled 
in Q1 ’25, gaining popularity thanks to their ~$20K price points in a vehicle market challenged by affordability issues. On the other end of the 
spectrum, luxury purchases may have been pulled forward into Q1 in anticipation of tariffs on imported luxury vehicles, as implied by the 
strong Porsche and JLR gains.  
The sales outlook for the remainder of 2025 remains murky, obscured by ever-changing trade policy and nagging customer affordability 
concerns. If tariffs do take hold and increase the cost of imported vehicles, we would anticipate a significant shift in sales share in favor of 
OEMs with more domestic production.

The average publicly owned auto dealership generated an estimated $3,135 in front-end gross profit per new vehicle retailed in Q1 2025.  This 
level of profit per new vehicle (“PNR”) is 65.1% higher than in 2019, but down 4.9% quarter-over-quarter and 14.4% year-over-year.  In Q1, there 
were a lot of factors influencing new vehicle pricing and subsequent gross profits.  In terms of headwinds, winter storms kept customers 
home in many parts of the country, suppressing demand. Many EVs still required significant discounting in order to sell, eating away at front-
end gross profits.  And some OEMs terminated incentive programs in March and April, further eroding dealer profitability. The major tailwind 
for new vehicle gross profits in Q1 was a significant uptick in demand beginning in March as consumers proactively purchased vehicles to get 
ahead of President Trump’s tariffs.  Gross profits on new vehicles spiked during the pandemic but have been edging back down over the past 
24 months or so. The rate of decline has been slowing and may be nearing a floor. Bank of America’s John Murphy pointed this out during 
the Asbury Automotive Q1 earnings call when he said, “You’re kind of reaching what appears to be an asymptotic limit on the downside.” For 
those of you rusty on your geometry, this means margins on new cars may not decline much further.
New Vehicle Gross Profits Slide 14% Year-over-Year
In Q1 2025, the average publicly owned auto dealership earned an average of $1,642 in front-end gross profit per used vehicle retailed (“PVR”).  
This used gross profit PVR represents an 8.5% increase quarter-over-quarter and a 4.1% increase year-over-year.  Used vehicle demand 
accelerated heavily in March, rising 19.4% month-over-month according to Cox Automotive’s vAuto.
As the effects of these tariffs creep from headlines into window stickers, the higher expected cost of new vehicles has already begun to pique 
increased demand for used vehicles, and that may give dealers more ability to command higher gross profits on used vehicles.  
But taking advantage of a bolstered used vehicle market will not be easy. Many dealer groups, including the publics, are struggling with a 
dearth of desirable used vehicle inventory – used vehicles between one and four years old.  In their first quarter earnings calls, both Sonic 
and AutoNation stated that used vehicles were a priority going forward in 2025, with the former buying a greater percentage of vehicles off 
the street and the latter attempting to increase its used vehicle inventory outright.

F&I Profit Grows, Nears Historic Peak
In the first quarter of 2025, the average publicly owned auto dealership earned $2,505 in finance and insurance (“F&I”) gross profit per vehicle 
retailed. The F&I PVR recorded in Q1, which is nearly at a historic high, represented a 0.1% increase quarter-over-quarter and a 2.8% increase 
year-over-year. According to the public dealership groups, this improvement can be attributed to improving the number of F&I products 
sold per vehicle.
Fixed Operations Profits Boomed in Q1
Fixed operations gross profit increased 5.8% for the publicly traded 
auto retailers from Q1 2024 to the first quarter of 2025; one of the 
fastest growth periods on record. The average number of vehicles 
on the road has continued to grow which is fueling part of this 
increase. Also, an increasing number of EV repair orders, which 
are roughly twice as high as comparable ICE RO’s, boosted fixed 
gross profit in Q1. While discussing their Q1 growth on their earnings 
calls, public company leaders highlighted their focus on technician 
hiring and retention, with Penske’s technicians anecdotally grossing 
$30,000 per month. Some large dealer groups, like AutoNation, are 
investing significantly in mobile service to continue boosting their 
fixed operations in 2025.
Looking ahead to the end of the year, we anticipate continued 
robust growth in fixed operations, particularly if tariffs have a 
significant impact on new vehicle pricing. With new vehicle 
affordability already an issue and used vehicle affordability poised 
to become a challenge as well, consumers will likely be forced to 
continue repairing their current vehicles if they are priced out of 
purchasing a replacement. Another factor that could influence 
fixed operations gross profits at dealerships throughout the rest 
of the year is tariffs on imported auto parts. If dealers find that 
customers can absorb much of the increased parts cost, their fixed 
operations departments may be in for a highly lucrative year if they 
can hold margin.

GM: A Product Renaissance 
General Motors is firing on all cylinders and whirring on all electric motors. In Q1 2025, the All-American OEM posted a 17% sales gain, including 
double-digit sales growth for all four of its brands. This sales surge allowed GM to pick up 2% more market share in Q1.   
Many dealership operations depend upon the sale of new vehicles. Want to sell more used cars? Sell more new cars.  Want to sell more parts? 
Sell more new cars.  So, when an OEM hits its stride like GM has, and customers are pouring into showrooms to check out the latest products, 
that demand then lifts all departments within the dealership. This combinatory effect then leads to higher dealership profits. So, we would 
like to send our congratulations and a collective industry “thank you” to Mary Barra and her team at GM. The Heartbeat of America is strong 
today! 
Readers of this report may have seen GM’s success coming. Since 2023, we have increased the multiples of every one of GM’s four brands.  
Dealers have become increasingly bullish on the future earnings prospects of all GM dealerships, which have translated into stronger offers 
on GM dealerships when they go up for sale.  We like to be right, and we also like it when dealers win. Who doesn’t like to have their cake, and 
eat it, too?
While we commend GM on its strong performance, its recent handling of the 6.2L V8 recall could have been better. Poor communication from 
GM regarding the recall of their flagship 6.2L V8 led to dealers being unprepared to handle a growing number of highly frustrated consumers.  
And some frustrated consumers gave up their troubled vehicles and moved to other brands. Had GM adopted the Toyota standard of engine 
replacements, the issue would likely be resolved by now, and GM could have increased customer loyalty and residual values.

Porsche
Porsche sales soared 40.6% in Q1 2025, claiming the best first quarter 
sales gain of the franchises we track. Porsche stores continue to 
be in high demand due to their scarcity, sexy products and high 
margins. We have some dealer friends that will buy a Porsche 
store anywhere in the country. But there are some dealers that are 
beginning to grumble about the difficulty of dealing with Porsche 
management. Dealers tell us the facility demands are extremely 
expensive and unnecessary. Large customer lounges and cafes 
are ghost towns (Porsche customers don’t typically wait around 
for service work to be completed), and dealers are increasingly 
irritated by Porsche micromanagement. The 911s sell extremely well 
and dealers fight to get more allocation, but the other models are 
often hard to sell, particularly the new Macan EV. These issues are 
all fixable, however, and the Porsche brand remains powerful and 
aspirational to many dealers as well as customers. Same multiple 
range: 9.0x-10.0x.
Lexus
Lexus achieved its best-ever first quarter in the U.S., with 83,043 
vehicles sold, a 5.8% increase year over year. Lexus dealers think 
the brand is on track to take the luxury crown in 2025, passing 
BMW. Electrified vehicles accounted for 13.1% of overall sales. The 
brand has always had the RX crossover that customers love, and 
now they have the TX and TX hybrids, which are powerful, efficient, 
and luxurious. Add the new LX, LX hybrid, and GX to the model line, 
and dealers are impressed. There was a time when the product 
cadence at Lexus was pedestrian, and dealers complained year 
after year (for example - comparing the GS350 to the Germans), 
and they were not happy. Today, Lexus dealers are excited that the 
division rationalized some models and refreshed two important 
models. Lexus dealers report a shortage of inventory and limited

holding gross like they did during COVID. Only the Lexus ES and TX 
are made in the U.S., which has some dealers concerned.  However,

navigate and be supportive during these changes. The only issue 
for Lexus dealers is inventory. Just send more… Same multiple

Mercedes-Benz sales were down 9.2% in Q1 2025 compared to

OEMs. Acura and Honda have significant domestic production

Subaru
Subaru dealers report sales up 9.1% in Q1 2025 over Q1 2024. Rural 
locations are doing particularly well at holding margins, while their 
urban counterparts are experiencing modest margin compression. 
Fixed operations remain flat compared to the previous quarter, 
indicating consistent performance. A standout highlight has been 
the launch of the Forester Hybrid, which is performing exceptionally 
well. Several dealers noted they’ve maintained a zero-day supply 
since its debut. Subaru customers are calling for more hybrids and 
EVs. Subaru’s factory support remains consistent, with no major 
changes to incentive programs or policies. The OEM has also 
indicated it intends to absorb the majority of any cost increases 
due to tariffs for the time being. Dealer sentiment remains positive 
overall, bolstered by Subaru’s strong customer loyalty and brand 
equity. Most believe any negative impacts from tariffs will be 
limited and short-lived. Same multiple range: 5.5x-6.5x.
Kia
Demand for Kia dealerships may be at an all-time high. Kia sales 
increased 10.7% in Q1 2025 and the OEM extended its record-
breaking sales streak to six months in March. Kia claims that retail 
transactions grew 25% year-over-year at Kia dealerships at the 
end of the first quarter. In a sign of the times, dealers we spoke 
to for this report stated that they would rather acquire a high-
volume Kia dealership than a high-volume Audi dealership, a 
comparison that would have been absurd five to ten years ago.     
U.S. consumers love the styling, features, pricing and powertrain 
options provided by Kia’s current lineup.  And U.S. Kia dealers praise 
the management team. As Kia units in operation grow, so too 
do fixed operations. Anecdotally, fixed operations performance 
at Kia dealerships is catching up to that of comparable Honda 
dealerships. This is particularly impressive given how strong Honda 
dealerships tend to perform in fixed. Adding to the strength of the 
brand, Kia is investing significantly in U.S. production, having just 
opened its new Metaplant America facility in Georgia at a cost of 
$7.59B. Localized production will help to insulate the brand from 
tariffs moving forward. Same multiple range: 4.5x-5.5x.
Hyundai / Genesis
Like Kia, Hyundai enjoyed a double-digit sales bump in Q1 2025, 
with brand sales increasing 10.3% over Q1 2024. Also like Kia, Hyundai 
set its sixth consecutive monthly sales record in March. Hyundai is 
investing $20.5B in domestic production and will also benefit from 
the recently opened Metaplant America facility in Georgia that it 
shares with Kia, reducing downside tariff risk for dealers. Tucson, 
Sante Fe, Elantra N and IONIQ 5 sales contributed to the bulk of 
the growth, with electrified vehicles representing 26% of Q1 sales.  
Despite the major and continued growth of Hyundai and Genesis, 
demand for these dealerships remains below the demand for 
Kia, Honda, Subaru or Toyota stores. Existing dealers tell us that 
Hyundai/Genesis management is difficult to work with, and buyers 
seeking to acquire Hyundai stores often experience challenges 
in gaining approval during a buy-sell. Some won’t try again...  
Despite being nearly identical to Kia, Hyundai dealerships trade at 
a discount, largely due to the difficulty of acquiring them. Same 
multiple range: 3.75x-4.75x.
Mazda
Mazda dealers remain excited about the future with the continued 
momentum of the brand. Mazda had a best-ever 2024 and started 
2025 with a record Q1 selling over 110,000, which is a 10.2% increase 
year over year. The heart of the brand is the CX-50, which is made 
in Huntsville, Alabama, and the CX-90. Mazda achieved a best-ever 
March with over 43K units sold, and this was the best ever for CX-50, 
CX-70, and CX-90. Mazda has the product mix, performance, and 
designs that resonate with customers, although it could benefit 
from hybrids. Dealers are excited about the brand’s momentum 
and overall support. One concern was the volume of imported 
vehicles vs competitors, but so far, the brand has continued to 
increase sales volume and has shown support in the face of tariffs.  
A recent dealer meeting in Texas had attendees excited about the 
brand’s future. Same multiple range: 3.75x-4.75x.
Nissan
Nissan sales rose 6.3% in Q1 2025. Nissan has a robust U.S. 
manufacturing presence with the Rogue, Altima, Pathfinder, Frontier 
and Murano all produced in either Tennessee or Mississippi. They 
are continuing with their Japan-built vehicles but they have made 
some adjustments to the Versa, Sentra and Kicks production that 
occurs in Mexico reducing trim levels and options that they are 
producing. The Nissan products that we saw at the New York Auto 
Show were attractively styled and loaded with features. Nissan 
is launching a “Get the Car You Want Tariff-Free” campaign that 
is highlighting their commitment to offering lower prices on the 
Rogue and Pathfinder. The new stair-step program kicked off 
last month that dealers are actually excited about because it is 
giving them decent margins on growing volume. The turnaround 
appears to have commenced. Same multiple range: 3.0x-4.0x.
Volkswagen
Volkswagen sold 7.1% more new vehicles in Q1 2025 than it did in Q1 
2024, a middle-of-the-pack performance among midline import 
franchises.  Sentiment across the network was mostly unchanged 
from Q4 2024. VW dealers are excited by upcoming product, 
including the ID. Buzz, which promptly had a major recall (the rear 
seat was too wide?). Average throughput per location is about 50 
new vehicles per month, similar to franchises like Chevrolet and 
Audi. But a lot of frustration remains present in the channel. VW 
is highly exposed to potential tariff risk, and many of its leading 
dealers are a part of lawsuits against the OEM’s decision to sell 
its Scout brand direct-to-consumer. We wonder if this plan will be 
put on hold so that VW can repurpose the factory that is under 
construction from building Scout products to building Audi and/or 
VW products. Electric trucks are not performing well in the market 
right now. Same multiple range: 3.0x-4.0x.

Chevrolet
Chevrolet dealers remain pleased with strong sales and strong 
profits.  Chevrolet sales grew 14.2% and the brand gained a full point 
of market share – not an easy accomplishment and a welcome 
reversal from years of lost share. Dealers tell us they believe 
Chevrolet has the best model lineup they have ever seen. But not 
everything is perfect: dealers are currently frustrated by stop-sale 
orders on 6.2L V8 models, which are typically the highest-grossing 
units.  Big recalls like this are usually great for dealer service shops, 
but at time of this writing, there were no specifics on how the 
recall would be handled. Larger Chevrolet dealers are upbeat and 
encouraged with Chevrolet’s product lineup and market share 
gains, while smaller dealers complain they are at an allocation 
disadvantage and have difficulty growing their businesses under 
GM’s current system. Same multiple range: 3.75x-4.75x.
Ford
Ford is the best positioned OEM to deal with tariffs since it has 
the highest percentage of its products made in the US. Ford 
dealers have responded positively to the “America First Pricing” 
strategy, which has also resonated extremely well with consumers.  
This program provided several benefits to Ford and its dealer 
network: boosting market share while reducing days’ supply and 
dissuading dealers from pricing vehicles above MSRP.    But despite 
the excitement around promotional pricing, Ford dealers still have 
a few concerns.  Inventory levels are still not quite where they need 
to be, and many dealers expect that the program will be extended 
through July. The F-Series and other trucks continue to serve as the 
core profit drivers for Ford retailers. However, enthusiasm for EVs 
has waned significantly, with many dealers reporting a plateau in 
EV sales and no significant gains in market share. Ford’s ordering 
and program systems also continue to frustrate dealers, with 
complexity still cited as a key barrier to efficiency. That said, there 
are efforts underway to simplify processes. Finally, demand remains 
strong for the Maverick, though supply constraints continue to 
limit availability. The brand remains desirable and dealers are 
optimistic about the future, especially with Ford’s advantage if the 
tariffs stick. Same multiple range: 3.5x-4.5x.
Buick-GMC
Buick and GMC dealers fared even better than their Chevrolet-
dealing cousins, posting a 23.3% gain in sales for Q1. Combined 
Buick-GMC gained 0.8% market share...almost a full point despite 
being much smaller than Chevy. Buick sales were up 39.3% as this 
brand continues to be on fire. With another 135 franchises returned 
to GM last year, throughput for the average Buick point is up 65%.  
Given the stellar results over the last few years, it is no surprise that 
GM’s former head of Buick-GMC was recently named president 
of North America, a move widely applauded by dealers. GMC is 
going through similar issues with the engine recall...stop sales are 
frustrating but fixed operations benefit could be lucrative when the 
fix is provided. Same multiple range: 3.5x-4.5x.
Stellantis
In our Q4 report, we showed how Stellantis average transaction 
prices (“ATP”) had risen much faster than the market during 2022 
and 2023, playing a big role in significant market share loss.  We 
illustrated how their pricing was improving at the end of last 
year and now we are pleased to report that pricing discipline is 
continuing to improve.  Headlines reported a 12% drop in Stellantis 
sales during Q1, but this was primarily driven by a 35% drop in fleet 
sales. Dealers are reporting improvements in their days’ supply of 
new vehicles and are encouraged that a leadership change will 
be a positive for the dealer network. Dealers share that recently 
launched employee pricing incentives should help but new 
product is needed in order to be regain share. Many core Stellantis 
models are old. Some dealer groups continue to maintain a 
moratorium on buying Stellantis stores, while others believe the 
trough was reached in 2024 and would consider buying into the 
brand now as a value investment.  Buying a Stellantis store in 2025 
or 2026 could prove to be a good long-term investment. Same 
multiple range: 3.0x-4.0x.
