The impact of these new tariffs on PCBs manufactured in China cannot be ignored. With all boards now subject to an additional 20% duty starting March 4th—on top of the existing 25% for 6+ layer boards—companies relying on offshore PCB production will see significant cost increases. The exemption for 2- and 4-layer PCBs from the Section 301 tariffs is also set to expire in May, adding more uncertainty. For EMS providers, this reinforces the need to diversify supply chains and explore domestic or regional sourcing options.

    HS Code Showdown: Should a mechanical part of a smart phone made of plastics be in chapter 39 or chapter 85? - This is one of the most common debates in trade compliance: Where do purely mechanical plastic mobile phone parts—like casings, buttons, and SIM trays—belong in the HS code system? - The Core Conflict: Chapter 85 (Electrical Parts): Argues the items are parts of a Heading 8517 apparatus, specifically designed for that device. Chapter 39 (Plastics): Argues the items are fundamentally articles of plastic that perform only a passive, mechanical, or structural function. - The Prevailing Classification Answer Based on the General Interpretive Rules (GRI), specifically the exclusions in Section XVI Note 2, items that are themselves specifically described in another Chapter must be classified there. Since a plastic casing is a simple article of plastic, it is typically excluded from the electrical parts headings of Chapter 85. The correct classification is generally found under: ➡️ Chapter 39, Heading 3926 (Other articles of plastics). To classify under 8517.70, the part must be an integral electrical component (like an antenna or a printed circuit board), not just a passive plastic shell. What's your experience? Whether you're prototyping or mass-producing, smart sourcing helps avoid delays and control costs. Let’s break down how sourcing impacts your project. What Is Component Sourcing? It’s the process of procuring all electronic parts—resistors, ICs, connectors, sensors—needed for PCB assembly. This includes: - Identifying specs  - Choosing suppliers  - Negotiating pricing  - Managing orders and deliveries  The goal: Get the right parts, in the right quantity, at the right cost, on time. ⏳ How Sourcing Affects Lead Time -Component Availability: Common parts are off-the-shelf; specialized ones (e.g., automotive MCUs) can take 16+ weeks. One delayed component can halt your whole line. -Supplier Location: Local suppliers often mean faster shipping. International orders bring longer transit and customs risk. -Supply Chain Visibility: Suppliers with real-time tracking help you anticipate shortages and adjust schedules. -Alternate Sources: Relying on one supplier is risky. Pre-approved alternates save you when a part goes end-of-life or is unavailable. How Sourcing Impacts Cost -Part Prices: Costs vary by brand, specs, and quantity. High-demand components often carry premiums. -Logistics & MOQs: Shipping, import fees, and minimum order quantities add hidden costs. -Delays = Money Lost: Late parts mean factory downtime, rush fees, or last-minute redesigns. -Value Engineering: Sourcing experts can suggest equivalent but cheaper parts—cutting cost without cutting performance.
