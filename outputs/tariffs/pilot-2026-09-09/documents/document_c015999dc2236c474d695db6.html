The Manganese Frontier:

Asia, ASEAN, and the Global Battery Shift

Manganese is propelling a battery revolution across Asia and the ASEAN region, powering lithium-ion cells for electric vehicles (EVs) and energy storage through critical precursors like:

  1. high-purity manganese sulphate monohydrate (HPMSM),
  2. battery grade trimanganese tetraoxide (Mn₃O₄), and
  3. electrolytic manganese dioxide (EMD, MnO₂).

As Eurasia solidifies its dominance in manganese battery demand, U.S. tariffs in April 2025 send ripples worldwide, while China’s production prowess and policy shifts reshape supply chains.

This article explores these dynamics, spotlighting opportunities and challenges for Asian and ASEAN producers, Europe’s survival struggle, and strategic insights for stakeholders in a mass production market, increasingly- and soon to be driven by LMFP and Sodium-ion batteries, in conjunction with current LFP and NMC battery chemistries.

(Pure Solid state will enjoy smaller niche markets for another decade)

U.S. Tariffs: A Global Echo, Not the Epicenter

On April 2, 2025, the U.S. introduced a 10% baseline tariff on all imports, effective April 5, with rates for China soaring to 84% by April 9, combining baseline, reciprocal, and existing duties, as announced on April 8.

Exemptions in Annex II secure raw manganese inputs:

  • Manganese ores and concentrates (HTSUS 26020000).
  • Ferromanganese (HTSUS 72021110, 72021150, 72021910, 72021950).
  • Ferrosilicon manganese (HTSUS 72023000).
  • Unwrought manganese (HTSUS 81110047, 81110049).

Battery-grade precursors—HPMSM (likely HTSUS 2833.29.50), Mn₃O₄ (likely HTSUS 2820.90.00), and EMD (MnO₂, HTSUS 2820.10.00)—face these duties, impacting imports from China, which supplies over 90% of HPMSM and Mn₃O₄ globally.

While significant, the U.S. market for manganese-based batteries lags, with meaningful growth projected 5-8 years away. Asia and ASEAN, alongside broader Eurasia, are the current powerhouses, rendering U.S. tariffs a secondary factor in this region’s manganese narrative.

Asia and ASEAN: The Manganese demand Powerhouse

Eurasia, spearheaded by Asia, drives manganese battery demand.

China’s 90%+ control over HPMSM and Mn₃O₄ production anchors the region, supplying Japan, South Korea, and India’s surging EV markets.

ASEAN nations—Indonesia with its rich manganese reserves, Malaysia with processing ambitions, and Vietnam’s growing industrial base—are poised to lead.

U.S. tariffs inflate costs for Chinese exports—e.g., HPMSM from Australian ore—but ASEAN producers leverage tariff-free ore and proximity to Eurasian buyers.

On January 2, 2025, China proposed export restrictions on LMFP cathode technology, with consultation ending February 1, 2025, tightening its hold on next-gen materials. Its April 4 announcement of a 34% retaliatory tariff on U.S. goods, effective April 10, underscores the push for regional resilience.

Europe’s Survival Struggle

Europe grapples with a manganese crisis, torn between U.S. allegiance and its own energy and raw material imperatives. The 84% U.S. tariff on Chinese HPMSM disrupts supply chains for European battery makers, yet dependence on Chinese imports persists due to scant domestic production.

South Africa and Gabon, major medium to high grade Mn ore suppliers, offers relief, but Europe’s efforts—via the EU Critical Raw Materials Act—collide with U.S.-driven trade pressures.

With energy costs soaring and raw material security at risk, Europe’s battery sector fights to survive, while Asia’s market surges ahead.


Battery Evolution: Asia’s Next-Gen Edge

LMFP-, HLM (high-lithium & Mn) and Sodium-ion batteries, using different manganese precursors, may lead Eurasian demand soon—from China’s EV hubs to India’s emerging markets.

For LMFP, manganese precursors potential rank by volume (by 2030):

  1. HPMSM for its versatility,
  2. Mn₃O₄ for dry solid-phase CAM (cathode active material) production, and
  3. EMD (MnO₂) in smaller amounts.

Sodium-ion batteries may favor EMD in affordable precursors.

Unlike NMC811, where HPMSM costs were less critical, these chemistries demand low-cost production, pressing ASEAN producers to optimize for HPMSM, Mn₃O₄, and EMD to outpace LFP and sodium-ion competitors.

Strategic Considerations for the Eurasian Market

  • Producers:

Success requires flexible supply chains to navigate trade shifts, cost efficiency to meet Eurasian demand, and multi-product processing facilities to adapt to changes in supply-demand balances ---> fast.

Ironically,

  1. most Western HPMSM projects use ultra-low Mn-resources,
  2. with more Iron than Manganese in their resources,
  3. yet only produces one Mn product, (HPMSM)
  4. which is grossly over-supplied, and with 50% lower market price vs cost of production outside China,
  5. whilst virtually unlimited new HPMSM facilities which can be built, in less than 1 year, by Chinese operators with no issues getting funding, as soon as market prices double ($1600/mt +)


  • Investors:

Focus on projects with:

Adaptable Supply Chains: Resilience against global trade disruptions.

Low-Cost Production: Competitive economics for Asia’s price-driven market.

Vertical Integration: Control from ore to pCAM or CAM, to buffer policy risks.

Policy Independence: Minimal reliance on any nation’s regulations, or geopolitical whims.

Conclusion

  • As of April 9, 2025, U.S. tariffs—up to 84% on Chinese HPMSM, Mn₃O₄, and EMD—ripple globally, but Asia and ASEAN steer the manganese battery frontier.
  • Europe battles for survival, caught between U.S. ties, and raw material & energy needs, but
  • the U.S. lags as a major manganese battery market for another 5-8 years.
  • In Eurasia, low-cost, integrated producers must meet mid-nickel NMC, LMFP (HPMSM, Mn₃O₄, EMD) and sodium-ion demands.
  • Investors should back ventures with resilient supply chains, lean operations, captive regional markets, and independence from shifting policies—keys to thriving in this Asian-led manganese frontier.
