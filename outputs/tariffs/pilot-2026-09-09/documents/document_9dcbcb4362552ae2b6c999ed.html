Understanding the HS Code for Wire Harnesses

Introduction

 

Wire harnesses play a crucial role in modern technology, connecting electrical and electronic components in a wide array of applications, including automobiles, consumer electronics, industrial machinery, and aerospace systems. These intricate bundles of wires are essential for transmitting power and signal efficiently, making them a vital component in today's interconnected world. To facilitate global trade and harmonize product classification, the international community employs a standardized coding system known as the Harmonized System (HS) code. In this article, we will introduce and explore the HS code for wire harnesses, shedding light on its significance, and offering a comprehensive overview.

 

 

  1. The Harmonized System (HS) Code

 

The Harmonized System is an internationally recognized classification system used to classify goods traded across borders. It was developed by the World Customs Organization (WCO) to standardize product classification and facilitate international trade. The HS code comprises a series of numerical digits, organized hierarchically, providing a unique code for each product category. This system is widely used by customs authorities, logistics providers, and businesses to identify and categorize goods accurately during import and export processes.

 

  1. HS Code for Wire Harnesses

 

The HS code for wire harnesses falls under Chapter 85 of the Harmonized System, which covers electrical machinery and equipment, and parts thereof. More specifically, wire harnesses are classified under HS code 8544.30. This code represents "Insulated (including enameled or anodized) wire, cable (including coaxial cable), and other insulated electric conductors, whether or not fitted with connectors; optical fibre cables, made up of individually sheathed fibers, whether or not assembled with electric conductors or fitted with connectors."

 

  1. Breaking Down the HS Code for Wire Harnesses

 

To better understand the HS code for wire harnesses, let's break down its components.

  • 85: This represents the chapter number, indicating that the product falls under the category of electrical machinery and equipment.
  • 8544: The first four digits represent the heading number, narrowing down the classification to "Insulated electric conductors."
  • 8544.30: The fifth and sixth digits provide the subheading number, indicating "Other insulated electric conductors, whether or not fitted with connectors."

 

  1. Significance of the HS Code for Wire Harnesses

 

The HS code for wire harnesses serves several important purposes

  • Customs Clearance: When importing or exporting wire harnesses, the correct HS code ensures smooth customs clearance, as customs authorities can accurately identify the product and its associated regulations, duties, and taxes.
  • Trade Statistics: The HS code system enables governments and organizations to gather trade statistics for various products, including wire harnesses, helping to analyze market trends and make informed decisions.
  • Tariff Determination: The HS code is instrumental in determining the applicable tariff rates and trade policies, providing clarity for businesses involved in international trade.

 

 

What is the HS code for automobile wire harnesses in the USA?

 

In the United States, the HS code for automobile wire harnesses is 8544.30.00. This code falls under Chapter 85 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS), which covers electrical machinery and equipment, and parts thereof.

To break down the HS code for automobile wire harnesses in the USA:

  • 85: Represents the chapter number, indicating that the product falls under the category of electrical machinery and equipment.
  • 8544: The first four digits represent the heading number, narrowing down the classification to "Insulated (including enameled or anodized) wire, cable (including coaxial cable), and other insulated electric conductors, whether or not fitted with connectors; optical fiber cables, made up of individually sheathed fibers, whether or not assembled with electric conductors or fitted with connectors."
  • 8544.30: The fifth and sixth digits provide the subheading number, indicating "Other electric conductors, for a voltage not exceeding 1,000 volts; fitted with connectors."

 

 

What is the HS code for automobile wire harnesses in China?

 

The HS code for automobile wire harnesses in China is 8544.30.9000. However, please note that tariff codes are subject to updates and changes over time. It's essential to verify the latest HS code with the Chinese customs authorities or consult the latest version of the China Customs Import and Export Tariff of the People's Republic of China for the most accurate and up-to-date information, or you can check with your wire harness supplier to find out correct HS code.

To break down the HS code for automobile wire harnesses in China:

  • 85: Represents the chapter number, indicating that the product falls under the category of electrical machinery and equipment.
  • 8544: The first four digits represent the heading number, narrowing down the classification to "Insulated (including enameled or anodized) wire, cable (including coaxial cable), and other insulated electric conductors; optical fiber cables, made up of individually sheathed fibers, whether or not assembled with electric conductors or fitted with connectors."
  • 8544.30: The fifth and sixth digits provide the subheading number, indicating "Other electric conductors, for a voltage not exceeding 80 volts; fitted with connectors."
  • 9000: The seventh and eighth digits indicate a specific classification within the subheading, which might pertain to a particular type or variation of automobile wire harnesses.

US Wire Harness Tariff Rate

 

Wire harnesses imported into the United States are subject to tariffs determined by their classification under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS). Most wire harnesses fall under HTS codes ​8544.30.00 (vehicle-specific harnesses) or ​8544.42.00 (general electrical harnesses), with standard duty rates ranging from ​0% to 2.5%​, depending on their application and design. However, additional tariffs may apply based on geopolitical factors. For example, imports originating from China are often subject to ​Section 301 tariffs, which can add ​7.5% to 35%​ to the base rate, unless specific exclusions apply. Recent trade policies have maintained these tariffs, though businesses should monitor updates, as exemptions for certain products may periodically expire or be reinstated.

For precise rates, consult the ​USITC Tariff Database (hts.usitc.gov) or a licensed customs broker. Always verify the latest regulations, as trade policies and exemptions may change.
